# Sponsorship and Non-AdSense Revenue for Long-Form Narrative, Educational and Faceless YouTube Channels (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note on evidence quality. Three tiers are used throughout and labelled inline:
- **[VERIFIED DISCLOSURE]** = a creator, company or platform stating its own numbers (Kurzgesagt, Complexly/AP, Nebula Talent, Colin & Samir, Gospel Stats/Tubefilter, ChannelCrawler dataset, Collabstr marketplace data, Graphtreon tracker).
- **[MARKETPLACE / INDUSTRY AVERAGE]** = aggregated marketplace or trade-press data not tied to one creator.
- **[VENDOR CLAIM / ESTIMATE]** = rate calculators, agency blogs, SEO guides and "net worth" sites. Treated as planning ranges only; they frequently disagree with each other.
I found no 2025-2026 ThoughtLeaders, Passionfroot, Paladin, Izea or BBTV published rate study for education channels; the search engine returned ThoughtLeaders' newsletter and sales pages only. That is a gap, not an oversight.

## Key Question 1: Sponsorship CPM benchmarks 2025-2026 for integrated reads on education/history/science vs entertainment vs faceless/AI channels; flat-fee examples by channel size

### Takeaway
Across six independent 2025-2026 sources, integrated 60-90 second sponsorship reads on education/science channels cluster at roughly **$20-$40 per 1,000 views** (vendor ranges span $15-$50), with finance at $30-$100 and gaming/lifestyle/entertainment at $15-$30; no source publishes a separate "faceless" or "AI-narrated" CPM, and the only verified marketplace average (Collabstr: $255 realized per YouTube deal) shows how far the long tail sits below those benchmark CPMs.

### Cited Findings

**Integration CPM ranges by niche (2025-2026)**
- Influencer Marketing Hub (updated Aug 31, 2026): YouTube sponsorship CPMs "frequently fall between $15 and $30"; worked example 500,000 views x $20 CPM = ~$10,000; tech, finance, software and business education "often command higher rates" than lifestyle/entertainment; US/CA/UK/Western Europe audiences command higher rates. Cites Modash and ThoughtLeaders for deal structure, unnamed "benchmark surveys" for tiers. [MARKETPLACE / INDUSTRY AVERAGE] — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-influencer-rates)
- OutlierKit (2026): Education/Self-Improvement $20-$40 integration CPM (used as the 1.0x baseline); Finance $30-$60 (1.5-2.0x); Tech/Reviews $25-$45 (1.2-1.5x); Gaming $15-$30 (0.7-1.0x); Lifestyle/Vlog $15-$25 (0.7-0.9x). No history/science/documentary row. States its ranges are "planning estimates", not from a creator survey or brand deal sheet. [VENDOR CLAIM / ESTIMATE] — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)
- SponsorRadar: Science benchmarks at $20-$40 CPM, i.e. a channel averaging 50,000 views gets roughly $1,000-$2,000 per sponsored video; Education & productivity listed at $20-$40 CPM. [VENDOR CLAIM / ESTIMATE] — [SponsorRadar science](https://sponsorradar.com/youtube-sponsors/science); [SponsorRadar rates](https://sponsorradar.com/insights/youtube-sponsorship-rates-what-brands-should-pay)

Formula: (avg views / 1000) x CPM x multiplier. Source is Mediacube's own agency, not an external survey. [VENDOR CLAIM / ESTIMATE] — [Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/how-much-do-youtubers-get-paid-for-sponsored-videos-exclusive-interview)

No CPM/pricing data. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE of tracker data] — [Tubefilter newsletter](https://newsletter.tubefilter.com/p/youtube-sponsorships-are-up-54-we-know-because-we-now-track-them)
- ChannelCrawler YouTube Brand Visibility Report (Q1 2026, published Jun 4, 2026): 144.8M videos from 7.6M channels (>500 subs); 13.6M videos (9.4%) mention at least one brand; of curated brand associations 82.8% organic, 122,665 declared ads, 452,557 "likely sponsored", 28,678 affiliate-only; channels with 500-10K subs are the largest group mentioning brands. Categories with sharp Q1 2026 increases: AI, fintech, gaming, privacy, trading, consumer technology. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE of tracker data] — [ChannelCrawler](https://channelcrawler.com/insights/5-things-we-learned-from-the-youtube-sponsorship-landscape-report)
- ThoughtLeaders (Oct 29, 2026): between Sep 1 and Oct 27, 2026, 158 YouTube channels ran a Whatnot sponsorship in at least one long-form video, far more than the same weeks of 2025. [MARKETPLACE / INDUSTRY AVERAGE] — [ThoughtLeaders](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/weekly-insights-newsletter/youtube-sold-112b-of-ads-what-it-means-for-buyers)

### Inferences
- Taking the median of the six 2025-2026 sources, a planning CPM for an integrated read on an education/history/science channel is ~$25-$35 per 1,000 views on the sponsored video, with $20 as a floor for channels without a sales agent and $40-$50 for channels with strong US/UK demographics or finance-adjacent topics.
- Implied average views per sponsored video from Gospel Stats = 19.1B / 65,759 ≈ 290,000, consistent with its "middle class" finding; at a $25 CPM that implies a typical integration fee of ~$7,000, which sits inside the Influencer Marketing Hub mid-tier range.
- The Collabstr $255 average vs. the $1,000-$10,000 mid-tier benchmark shows the benchmark CPMs describe channels that have reached ~50K+ views per video and are actively sold; a marketplace-listed long-tail channel earns a fraction of that.
- No source prices faceless/AI-narrated reads separately; vendor guides apply the same views x CPM formula. The absence of a published discount is itself a signal that pricing is niche- and audience-driven rather than format-driven, but see Key Question 2 for demand-side caveats.

### Gaps
- No published 2025-2026 ThoughtLeaders, Passionfroot, Paladin, Izea, BBTV or Grapevine rate table by category was surfaced; ThoughtLeaders' deal data appears to be behind its platform login.
- No source gives a documentary- or history-specific CPM; the nearest are "science" and "education".
- No verified (creator-disclosed) flat fee for a 100K/500K/1M-sub narrative channel was found; all tier figures are marketplace or vendor ranges.

## Key Question 2: Which sponsors buy on faceless/AI-narrated channels, and do 2026 brand guidelines exclude AI-generated or faceless content?

### Takeaway
Trade reporting (Digiday, Marketing Brew) says advertiser interest in faceless creators is rising but the majority of branded-content spend still goes to on-camera long-form creators, and YouTube offers advertisers no opt-out for AI-produced content; Nebula Talent's public sponsor roster (NordVPN, Ground News, Brilliant, Incogni, Skillshare, etc.) confirms the DTC/VPN/edtech sponsor set buys heavily on narrated explainer channels, while claims that brands now require "human voice" or exclude faceless channels come only from unsourced vendor sites.

### Cited Findings

**Sponsor set active on narrated/explainer channels**
- Nebula Talent (the Standard/Nebula sales arm for 200+ creators, most of them documentary/explainer channels) lists these sponsors on its public page: NordVPN, Ground (Ground News), Brilliant, Henson Shaving, 80,000 Hours, Adam & Eve, Akiflow, Amboss, Antlion, Aura, Bellroy, Clarendon, Copilot, Factor, GiveWell, Grammarly, HelloFresh, Imprint, Incogni, Insta360, Keysight, MUBI, NordPass, Onshape, Pulseway, Raycon, Rocket, Rovio, Skillshare, Upperstory, World Anvil, Wren; describes them as "highly reputable sponsors like Brilliant and Skillshare"; requires sponsors to share performance data and work with creators long term. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE] — [Nebula Talent](https://talent.nebula.tv)
- SponsorRadar: Nebula itself has 177 tracked YouTube sponsorship deals across 45 channels since Nov 2021, and is among the most active sponsors of science channels alongside Brilliant.org. [VENDOR tracker data] — [SponsorRadar Nebula brand](https://sponsorradar.com/brands/nebula-1); [SponsorRadar science](https://sponsorradar.com/youtube-sponsors/science)
- ChannelCrawler Q1 2026: fastest-growing creator-mention categories are AI, fintech, gaming, privacy (VPN/data-removal), trading and consumer tech. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE of tracker data] — [ChannelCrawler](https://channelcrawler.com/insights/5-things-we-learned-from-the-youtube-sponsorship-landscape-report)
- Vendor guides claim finance brands, SaaS companies and e-commerce stores "regularly sponsor faceless channels in relevant niches" and that "brands mostly care about audience fit, not whether a creator appears on screen"; another says brands are "curious but cautious" about faceless pages. [VENDOR CLAIM] — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/faceless-youtube-sponsorships-guide); [Influencers Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/youtube-faceless-creator-crackdown-ai-content-suppression-gu/)

**Advertiser attitude evidence (trade press)**

[MARKETPLACE / INDUSTRY reporting] — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-has-creators-concerned-marketers-cheering/)

[MARKETPLACE / INDUSTRY reporting] — [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/07/14/ai-slop-proliferating-on-youtube-advertisements)
- Digiday: Zefr's chief commercial officer calls AI video "the next big brand safety problem"; Zefr built a tool to track AI-generated material in campaigns. [MARKETPLACE / INDUSTRY reporting] — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/future-of-marketing-briefing-marketers-confront-a-new-kind-of-brand-safety-problem-in-ai-video/)

[MARKETPLACE / INDUSTRY reporting] — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-creator-economy/)

- YouTube policy context: vendor guides agree YouTube's July 2025 and January 2026 enforcement targets templated mass-produced uploads (e.g., channels posting 5-10 identical-template AI videos/day terminated in Jan 2026), not narrated original content; "a narrated, edited, original video qualifies whether a human or an AI did the narrating." [VENDOR CLAIM] — [Eliro](https://eliro.pro/blog/is-faceless-youtube-dead-2026); [Vizard](https://vizard.ai/blog/youtubes-crackdown-on-ai-slop-is-reshaping-the-faceless-creator-economy)

### Inferences
- The Nebula sponsor roster is the strongest verified evidence that the VPN / data-removal (Incogni, Aura, NordVPN, NordPass) / edtech (Brilliant, Skillshare, Imprint) / news (Ground) / meal-kit (HelloFresh, Factor) sponsor set buys at scale on narrated explainer channels — many Nebula channels (Wendover, RealLifeLore, Half as Interesting, PolyMatter) are human-narrated and effectively faceless (background knowledge, not from a fetched source). The distinction brands appear to draw is human-written, human-narrated, original vs. AI-mass-produced, not on-camera vs. voiceover.
- No sponsorship marketplace (Passionfroot, Paladin, ThoughtLeaders, Collabstr) was found publishing a policy excluding faceless or AI-voiced channels; brands instead self-select through performance data (Nebula requires sponsors to share conversion data), which penalizes channels with weak audience trust rather than faceless format per se.
- Fully AI-narrated channels should expect to price at the bottom of the niche band and to face more rejections from brand-safety-sensitive advertisers (Zefr clients) than human-narrated ones; this is inferred from the Digiday/Zefr/Marketing Brew reporting, not from a published rate differential.

### Gaps
- No 2026 brand guideline document, agency policy or marketplace terms-of-service explicitly excluding AI-generated or faceless content was found.
- No quantitative split of sponsorship spend between on-camera and faceless channels exists in the sources (Digiday says "most" with no number).
- No Raptive or The Current (Trade Desk) piece specific to YouTube creator sponsorships and AI content surfaced in searches.

## Key Question 3: Ratio of sponsorship to AdSense revenue reported by education creators

### Takeaway
The only full public disclosures from education studios show AdSense at 10-33% of total revenue: Kurzgesagt reported YouTube ads at 13% (2020-2022) vs. commercial sponsors ~11%, institutional grants ~13%, shop 45%, Patreon 7%; Complexly (Crash Course/SciShow) went from "about one-third" AdSense in Jan 2022 to "about one-tenth" AdSense and one-tenth Patreon in Feb 2026 as philanthropy ($4.8M in 2025) grew; creator-business channel Colin & Samir report AdSense under 10% with brand sponsorships the main driver.

### Cited Findings
- Kurzgesagt (official Jan 2023 statement by staff on conflict-of-interest allegations), covering roughly the prior three years: "our shop accounted for 45% of our revenue, YouTube ads 13%, and Patreon 7%. So this means 65% of our revenue came directly from our viewers. In the last three years, the second biggest chunk was money from commercial partners advertising products – around 11% of our revenue." A staff reply in the same thread gives institutional sponsorships and grants 13% and licensing/agency work 11%. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE, 2020-2022 data] — [Kurzgesagt Medium statement](https://medium.com/@Kurzgesagt/kurzgesagt-sponsorships-on-youtube-3121a45b0fe9) (returned 403 on fetch; quote taken from search index); [Reddit staff comment mirror](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/kurzgesagt/comments/10jlyyk/kurzgesagt_statement_to_the_conflict_of_interest/j7qqg8g); Wikipedia summarizes it as 65% viewer-driven (shop + Patreon + AdSense) and 24% sponsorships and grants for 2020-2022 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurzgesagt)
- Kurzgesagt "How We Make Money on YouTube with 20M Subs" video (summary, ~8-year view): shop 40%, YouTube ads 13%, Patreon 9%, commercial sponsors 12%, institutional sponsors 10%. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE via third-party transcript summary] — [VideoHighlight summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/1x-i9z617z4)
- Kurzgesagt institutional funding: Gates Foundation $570,000 (2015); Open Philanthropy EUR 2.97M (Mar 2022) for translations and TikTok; John Templeton Foundation (amount not given); German public broadcaster network Funk funded the German branch Sep 2017-Jan 2023. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurzgesagt)
- Complexly (Hank Green video, reported by Tubefilter Jan 24, 2022): YouTube Partner Program supplies "about one-third" of Complexly's total revenue. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE, 2022] — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=164850)
- Complexly (AP, Feb 4, 2026, on conversion to nonprofit): about one-tenth of revenue from YouTube's ad-share program; Complexly estimates about one-tenth from Patreon (patrons typically $5 or $10/month); $4.8 million philanthropic funding in 2025 (YouTube, PBS, Alfred P. Sloan Foundation; ASU and HHMI underwrite Crash Course projects); committing $8.5M to new content; annual silver Crash Course coin sales; CEO Julie Walsh Smith says no paywalls ever. Total revenue not disclosed. [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE, 2026] — [AP via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainics/articles/hank-john-greens-studio-becomes-131718647.html)
- Colin & Samir (creator-economy show, ~125K subs at the time): Samir Chaudry to Digiday — "AdSense ... should make up about 10%. And 30% is speaking, consulting and creative projects"; $268,000 AdSense in 2022. In a later Q&A video they say AdSense is "less than 10%" of the business, which is "driven primarily by brand sponsorships." [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE, 2022-2023; not an education channel but a long-form talk format] — [Digiday Q&A](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/future-of-tv-briefing-a-qa-with-colin-and-samirs-samir-chaudry-on-the-state-of-the-creator-economy); [Colin & Samir video](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/youtubers-ask-us-tough-questions)
- Johnny Harris (May 11, 2025, Tara Palmeri profile): 6.6M subscribers, staff of 30+, overhead of $100,000/month, described as "making millions of dollars per year on YouTube through AdSense"; no sponsorship split disclosed. [PARTIAL DISCLOSURE, journalist paraphrase] — [Tara Palmeri](https://www.tarapalmeri.com/p/youtube-star-johnny-harris-on-his)
- Third-party "net worth" sites give Johnny Harris $2M net worth or $3M/year and Hank Green $16M-$30M; none itemizes sponsorship vs AdSense and all are modeled. [VENDOR ESTIMATE, unreliable] — [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/influencer/johnny-harris/); [NetworthSpot](https://www.networthspot.com/johnny-harris/net-worth/)

### Inferences
- For a mature education studio, sponsorships (commercial + institutional) are roughly 1.0-2.0x AdSense: Kurzgesagt 24% vs 13% (1.8x); Complexly 2026 implies philanthropy + sponsorship dwarf the ~10% AdSense share (grants alone were $4.8M).
- Non-ad share of total revenue in every verified disclosure is 67-90% (Kurzgesagt 87%, Complexly 2026 ~90%, Complexly 2022 ~67%, Colin & Samir >90%). A well-run narrative channel without a merch or grant engine will sit lower (see Key Question 7 synthesis), but the verified floor for non-ad share among disclosing education creators is two-thirds.
- Kurzgesagt is an outlier on merch (40-45%); a story/history channel without a design-led shop should not model that line above ~5-10%.

### Gaps
- No verified sponsorship-vs-AdSense breakdown for Wendover Productions, Real Engineering, Johnny Harris or any pure history/story channel was found; Nebula-affiliated creators do not publish splits.
- Complexly total revenue is undisclosed, so the one-tenth figures cannot be converted to dollars.

## Key Question 4: Patreon / channel-membership conversion and ARPU for documentary/history channels; Nebula revenue share

### Takeaway
Patreon's own stated conversion from off-platform audience to paying members is 0.005%-2.5% (third-party planning guides cluster at 0.5-2%), typical pledges for education shows are $5-$10/month (Complexly), and a large animated-history channel (Kings and Generals) currently has ~2,000 patrons worth an estimated $3K-$16K/month; Nebula pays creators via a 50/50 monthly profit split allocated by watch time plus 20% commission on managed sponsorships, with no public per-creator payout figures.

### Cited Findings
- Patreon Creator Hub: "Our average conversion... from off platform subscribers or people who are consuming your content regularly to actually becoming a Patreon member is anywhere between 0.005% and 2.5%." [VERIFIED DISCLOSURE; quote taken from search index — the page redirected and the destination returned 403] — [Patreon Creator Hub](https://creatorhub.patreon.com/articles/the-art-and-science-of-using-your-patreon-data)
- Third-party planning ranges: 0.5-2% of subscribers; 0.1-0.7%; 1-3% of existing audience; one blog claims ~5% (conflicts with Patreon's range). One guide claims higher conversion in "niches with passionate communities (history, true crime, niche science, geopolitics)" — asserted, not measured. [VENDOR CLAIM] — [Creator Revenue Calculator](https://creatorrevenuecalculator.com/blog/how-a-patreon-tier-ladder-converts-free-fans-to-paid-subscribers/); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/patreon-youtube-faceless-creator); [YTGrowth](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-channel-memberships)

### Inferences
- Using Patreon's $5-$10 typical pledge and Graphtreon's ~$6/patron implied average, net ARPU after Patreon's 10% fee and payment processing is roughly $4.50-$8.50 per patron per month.
- Worked example for a 300K-sub history channel at 0.5-1.0% conversion: 1,500-3,000 patrons x ~$6 net ≈ $9K-$18K/month. At 1.5M monthly views that is ~$6-$12 per 1,000 views — comparable to or above education AdSense RPM. Kurzgesagt's own ratio (Patreon 7% vs ads 13%) suggests Patreon ≈ 50-55% of AdSense for a channel that does not push memberships hard.
- Kings and Generals' ~2,000 patrons on a multi-million-subscriber channel implies a conversion rate well under 0.1% of subscribers (subscriber count not fetched, so not computed precisely), i.e., mass-reach history channels sit at the low end of Patreon's range; smaller, community-driven history podcasts/channels sit higher.
- Nebula income is a profit share, not a revenue share, so it is zero in loss months; the only guaranteed Nebula-related cash is the sponsorship a creator earns for advertising Nebula itself (177 tracked Nebula sponsorships across 45 channels per SponsorRadar).

### Gaps
- No niche-specific conversion rate or ARPU for documentary/history channels was published by Patreon or Graphtreon; the Patreon 0.005-2.5% range is all-niche.
- No per-creator Nebula payout figure has ever been disclosed in the sources found.
- Graphtreon pages for Fall of Civilizations and Historia Civilis were not fetched (search returned only Patreon landing pages).

## Key Question 6: Sponsorship marketplaces and agents — how a portfolio of ten 100K-300K-sub channels gets sponsorships at scale; take rates

### Takeaway
Agency commission on brand deals is consistently reported at 15-20% (range 10-25%, full-service management 20-25%), Nebula/Standard publishes a flat 20%, and the Standard/Nebula model (one sales team selling 200+ explainer channels to a shared sponsor roster, plus a bundled subscription product) is the closest verified template for cross-channel package selling; Spotter's pitch to brands is multi-year sponsorship packages with CTV reach as the value-add.

### Cited Findings
- Agency commission ranges: "YouTube talent agencies typically charge between 10% and 25%"; "most ... charge between 15% and 20%"; Creators Guild of America cited for full-service management at 20-25%; reputable agencies take commission only on deals they facilitate, and taking a cut of AdSense or merch they did not generate is flagged as a red flag; one roundup table lists 0-10% fees on AdSense (secondary). [AGENCY / VENDOR CLAIMS] — [Creators Agency commission](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/youtube-talent-agency-commission-rates-explained); [Creators Agency deal economics](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/how-talent-agencies-change-youtube-deal-economics); [InfluenceFlow](https://influenceflow.io/resources/influencer-talent-representation-complete-guide-for-creators-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- A ten-channel portfolio averaging 150K-300K views per video sits exactly in the Gospel Stats "middle class" where sponsorship volume grew 54% in 2025, which is favorable for fill rate.

An in-house sales desk at 20% effective cost competes with external agencies at 15-25%.
- Cross-channel package pricing should be modeled at the per-channel views x CPM formula summed, with the network's leverage coming from guaranteed monthly impression volume and multi-month retainers (OutlierKit lists mid-tier retainers at $10K-$60K/month per channel) rather than a published network premium — no source quantifies a bundle premium.

## Synthesis: Estimated total revenue per 1,000 views and non-ad share for a well-run narrative/education channel

### Takeaway
Combining the verified disclosures with the marketplace CPM bands, a well-run English-language narrative/education channel in the 100K-500K-views-per-video band can reasonably plan on $15-$45 of total revenue per 1,000 views, of which sponsorship contributes $10-$30 and memberships $3-$10, so the non-ad share is roughly 60-80%; a fully faceless/AI-narrated channel should plan on the bottom of each band and a lower sponsorship fill rate.

### Cited Findings
- All inputs are cited in Key Questions 1-6 above: sponsorship integration CPM $20-$40 (education/science) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/), [SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/youtube-sponsors/science), [Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/how-much-do-youtubers-get-paid-for-sponsored-videos-exclusive-interview), [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-influencer-rates); Patreon conversion 0.005-2.5% and $5-$10 pledges — [Patreon](https://creatorhub.patreon.com/articles/the-art-and-science-of-using-your-patreon-data), [AP](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/articles/hank-john-greens-studio-becomes-131718647.html); verified non-ad shares 67-90% — [Kurzgesagt via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurzgesagt), [Tubefilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=164850), [Colin & Samir via Digiday](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/future-of-tv-briefing-a-qa-with-colin-and-samirs-samir-chaudry-on-the-state-of-the-creator-economy).

### Inferences (modeled, not sourced as a whole)
- Sponsorship per 1,000 total channel views = integration CPM x share of videos carrying an integration. At $25-$35 CPM and 50-80% fill (fill rate is an assumption; no source measures it), effective sponsorship revenue is ~$12-$28 per 1,000 channel views. A channel with one agent-sold integration per video and strong US demographics reaches ~$30+.
- Memberships: ~$3-$10 per 1,000 views for channels converting 0.3-1% of subscribers at ~$6 net ARPU (worked example in Key Question 4).
- Merch/affiliate/audio: $0.50-$3 per 1,000 views absent an exceptional product (Kurzgesagt's 40-45% shop share is treated as an outlier).
- AdSense is covered by another researcher; for the ratio, the verified disclosures (Kurzgesagt 13%, Complexly 10-33%, Colin & Samir <10%) and the fact that sponsorship CPMs ($20-$40) exceed any plausible education ad RPM both point to non-ad revenue of 1.5-4x AdSense for a channel that sells its inventory. Non-ad share therefore ≈ 60-80% for a sponsored, member-supported narrative channel, and ≈ 85-90% at the Kurzgesagt/Complexly end.
- Faceless/AI-narrated adjustment: no published rate differential exists; based on Digiday's "most spend goes to on-camera creators" and the Zefr/IAS brand-safety signals, model fill rate at 30-60% and CPM at the $15-$25 floor, giving ~$5-$15 sponsorship per 1,000 views, so non-ad share falls toward 40-60% unless memberships compensate.

### Gaps
- No single source models total revenue per 1,000 views for a narrative channel; the figures above are this researcher's composition of cited inputs and should be presented as a modeled range.
- Sponsorship fill rate (share of videos with an integration) for education channels is undocumented in any source found.
