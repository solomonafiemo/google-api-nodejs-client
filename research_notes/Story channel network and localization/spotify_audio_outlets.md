# Spotify and Other Audio/Video-Podcast Outlets as a Monetization Channel for AI-Narrated Storytelling and Narrative-Education Content (as of November 2026)

Scope note: Research conducted 14 November 2026. Every policy and payout figure is dated. "Official" = Spotify/Apple/Amazon/YouTube/network documentation or newsroom; "anecdote" = creator self-report or secondary press. Several primary pages (Variety, Spotify Partner Program marketing page, a Medium creator essay, Yahoo/Engadget syndication) returned 403/404/429 and could not be fetched directly; where that happened the finding is attributed to the outlet that could be read.

## Key Question 1: Spotify Partner Program — eligibility, countries, payout mechanics, reported earnings, YouTube comparison

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026 the Spotify Partner Program (SPP) requires 3 published episodes, 2,000 Spotify consumption hours and 1,000 unique listeners/viewers in the trailing 30 days (lowered ~80% on 7 Jan 2026), is open in ~56 markets after the 20 Oct 2026 expansion, and pays (a) a 50% share of dynamic-ad revenue on Free-tier plays and (b) Premium video revenue via an undisclosed "proprietary formula" that weights consumption time and the viewer's registration market. Spotify publishes no per-1,000 or per-hour rate; the only public earnings data are Spotify aggregates ("hundreds" of creators >$10k/month; payouts +300% YoY in Jan 2025; monthly payouts +1/3 since Jan 2026) and creator claims (Chris Williamson: six figures/month, Spotify RPM "double to triple" YouTube's).

### Cited Findings

**Official eligibility and mechanics**
- Spotify's current support page lists the SPP criteria as: host on Spotify for Creators; legal address in an eligible market; at least 3 published episodes; "at least 2,000 consumption hours on Spotify in the last 30 days"; "at least 1,000 audience count on Spotify in the last 30 days"; criteria "may change at any time." To remain eligible a creator must "in any given 6 month period, have published an episode and have earned at least $10." — [Spotify Support: Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/)
- A stream counts toward eligibility and revenue only when a listener/viewer "streams your episode or video for 60 seconds or more." — [Spotify Support: Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/); also [Spotify Support: Partner Program analytics](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program-analytics/)
- The companion "Monetizing your show" page phrases the thresholds as "at least 2,000 global consumption hours in the last 30 days" and "at least 1,000 unique listeners or viewers on Spotify in the last 30 days," and notes that as of 20 Oct 2026 "Podcast Subscriptions have become Memberships" (Memberships require 3 episodes, 100 unique listeners and 100 consumption hours in 30 days; payouts via Stripe). Sponsorships (host-read ad management) require SPP enrollment and "only video episodes can be targeted with Sponsorships." — [Spotify Support: Monetizing your show](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/monetizing-your-show-with-spotify-for-creators/)
- 7 Jan 2026 newsroom post: thresholds reduced from 2,000 to 1,000 "engaged audience members, measured by plays over the last 30 days," from 10,000 to 2,000 hours, and from 12 to 3 episodes. Launch hosting partners on the Spotify Distribution API: Acast, Audioboom, Libsyn, Omny, Podigee. Sponsorship tools "rolling out starting April 2026 across Spotify for Creators and Megaphone." The post warns "Spotify Premium audiences may still experience breaks in video episodes due to creator sponsorships." — [Spotify Newsroom, 7 Jan 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-01-07/spotify-partner-program-updates)
- Ad revenue: creators earn "a 50% share of the revenue recognized" when Spotify-monetized ads play in their episodes on and off Spotify; at least one ad break must be inserted; creators can exclude ad categories. — [Spotify Support: Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/)
- Premium video revenue: applies when Premium members "in select markets" stream video episodes (no dynamic ads); calculated with a "proprietary payment formula" Spotify may adjust; stated factors are total qualifying consumption time of eligible video episodes, "the registration market of Premium users consuming those episodes," the unique number of Premium users with access to/consuming an uninterrupted video experience, and overall eligible video content consumed by Premium users in those markets. Video episodes without at least one ad break do not qualify. — [Spotify Support: Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/)
- Analytics definitions: "Ads RPM" = average earnings per 1,000 ad impressions over the last 30 days after the 50% split, excluding Premium video revenue; "Monthly earnings" = prior calendar month total incl. ad and Premium video revenue before fees/taxes; ads play only when advertiser demand exists for the show. "Spotify RPM" (Premium video) is referenced but not defined publicly. — [Spotify Support: Partner Program analytics](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program-analytics/)
- Payout admin: Spotify Payouts bank setup required; 2-step verification mandatory once earnings reach "$1,000 USD or more within one month," otherwise payouts are held after 30 days. Pausing >6 consecutive months may lead to removal. — [Spotify Support: Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/)
- Megaphone (Spotify's enterprise host) users must "talk to your representative to see if your organization is eligible"; other hosts should ask their provider. — [Spotify Support (regional mirror)](https://support.spotify.com/ca-en/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/)

### Inferences
- Because Premium video revenue scales with "qualifying consumption time" and audio-only episodes earn only the 50% ad share, a long-form narrated story/education show (high hours per listener) is structurally well suited to SPP — but only if published as video with at least one ad break inserted.
- Williamson's "2–3x YouTube RPM" claim, if applied to the $10 education RPM benchmark, would imply roughly $20–$30 per 1,000 Premium video views for a US-heavy audience; this is an inference from two unrelated sources, not a measured figure.
- Reaching the 2,000-hour threshold with 1,000 listeners requires ~2 hours of consumption per listener per month, which is easier for serialised fiction/sleep content than for short clips.

### Gaps
- Spotify publishes no Premium video rate per view or per hour; no creator was found publicly disclosing a dashboard "Spotify RPM" in dollars for 2025–2026.
- No disclosed earnings from Colin & Samir, Bella Fiori or Tim Gabel in dollar terms.
- Spotify's Partner Program marketing page (creators.spotify.com/partner-program) returned 404; the Monetization Policies and Partner Program Terms were not retrievable in this pass.

## Key Question 2: Spotify's policy on AI-generated content, synthetic voices and spam — music policy vs. podcasts

### Takeaway
Spotify's September 2025 AI framework (spam filter, impersonation ban, DDEX/"AI Credits" disclosure) applies to music; for podcasts, the only explicit rule (19 May 2026) is removal of shows that impersonate another creator's likeness via AI voice cloning or otherwise, plus "Verified by Spotify" badges. Spotify has not banned non-impersonating AI narration, has no mandatory AI-disclosure requirement for podcasts, and no documented case of an AI-narrated story podcast being removed or demonetized for using a synthetic voice was found; the documented 2026 removals were SEO/drug-spam fake podcasts.

### Cited Findings

**Music policy (25 Sep 2025 and one-year update 20 Oct 2026) — official/press**
- 25 Sep 2025: Spotify announced (1) a spam filter flagging tracks/uploaders using mass uploads, duplicates, SEO manipulation, artificially short tracks, and stopping their recommendation; (2) an impersonation policy prohibiting unauthorized AI voice clones/deepfakes; (3) support for a DDEX industry standard for AI disclosures in music credits (vocals, instrumentation, post-production), with 15 labels/distributors committed. Spotify said it removed 75M+ spammy tracks in the prior year and still "supports AI use provided it's used in a non-fraudulent way." — [TechCrunch, 25 Sep 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/spotify-updates-ai-policy-to-label-tracks-cut-down-on-spam); [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-has-deleted-75m-spammy-tracks-as-it-unveils-new-ai-music-policies)
- 20 Oct 2026 one-year update: >50M "slop and spam" tracks removed from recommendations this year; >75M removed from the platform; "AI Credits" launched April 2026 with "tens of thousands" submitted daily; "AI Personas" labeled and excluded from editorial/algorithmic recommendations by default; Artist Profile Protection beta to all artists by year-end; tracks with high probability of AI generation are 10x more likely to fall below the 1,000-stream royalty threshold. The post does not discuss podcasts or spoken word. — [Spotify Newsroom, 20 Oct 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-10-20/prioritizing-human-artistry/)
- Music-only rules: detected artificial streams earn no royalties and distributors are charged per track since 1 Apr 2024; tracks need 1,000 streams/12 months to earn royalties. No podcast equivalent was found. — [Spotify for Artists: Artificial Streaming](https://artists.spotify.com/artificial-streaming); [Spotify Support: Track monetization eligibility](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/track-monetization-eligibility/)

**Podcast policy (19 May 2026) — official via press**
- Spotify stated its "policies have always prohibited unauthorized impersonation" and that it "will remove podcast shows and content that impersonate another creator or host's likeness without permission, whether that's using AI voice cloning or any other method." "Verified by Spotify" light-green checkmark badge extended to podcasts; eligibility based on sustained listener activity, good standing with platform policies, and "verified audience authenticity" including bot safeguards; rollout across 7M+ titles "over the coming months"; described as "the first in a series of steps." — [Music Business Worldwide, 20 May 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-extends-verified-by-spotify-badges-to-podcasts-further-cracking-down-on-ai-impersonators/)
- Music Ally: "as with music, Spotify is not banning AI-generated podcasts outright." — [Music Ally, 20 May 2026](https://musically.com/2026/05/20/now-spotify-is-cracking-down-on-ai-impersonation-in-podcasts/) (summary via search; page returned 429 on direct fetch)
- The MBW article does not mention any disclosure requirement for AI podcasts and does not link badge eligibility to Partner Program monetization. — [Music Business Worldwide, 20 May 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-extends-verified-by-spotify-badges-to-podcasts-further-cracking-down-on-ai-impersonators/)
- RSS.com (hosting provider) guidance: Spotify "does not currently have a mandatory AI disclosure requirement for podcasts"; its AI labeling rules apply to music; creator guidance centers on not deceiving listeners, not violating IP, and maintaining human oversight. — [RSS.com Knowledge Base](https://help.rss.com/en/support/solutions/articles/44002518955-ai-generated-content-in-podcasting)
- Vendor claim (low reliability): "no AI-specific block on monetization"; shows are removed for copyright, voice impersonation without consent, or deception, not for using AI. — [Podcastify blog (vendor)](https://podcastify.io/blog/can-you-publish-ai-podcasts-on-spotify)
- Spotify's own support pages for the Partner Program and monetization contain no language on AI or synthetic content. — [Spotify Support: Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/); [Spotify Support: Monetizing your show](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/monetizing-your-show-with-spotify-for-creators/)

**Scale of AI podcasts and documented removals**
- Podcast Index data: AI-generated shows ~35.4% of new podcast feeds as of early May 2026 (one nine-day window 39%); Bloomberg reported one firm responsible for ~10,000 shows. — [Music Business Worldwide, 20 May 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-extends-verified-by-spotify-badges-to-podcasts-further-cracking-down-on-ai-impersonators/)
- Conflicting later reading: an earlier 44.6% figure was revised and a snapshot showed only 6.3% of shows launched in the prior 24 hours suspected AI-generated. — [Music Ally, 20 May 2026](https://musically.com/2026/05/20/now-spotify-is-cracking-down-on-ai-impersonation-in-podcasts/)
- June 2026: after a probe by Sen. Maggie Hassan, Spotify removed tens of thousands of fake podcast episodes promoting illegal online pharmacies (one outlet: 57,000 episodes, 94% with zero plays); Spotify called it a "spam attack" aimed at search-engine visibility; similar fakes found on iHeart and Amazon Music. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/spotify-removed-57000-fake-podcast-episodes-drug-spam); [Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/54e0203b_spotify_removed_tens/)
- No report was found of an AI-narrated podcast being demonetized or rejected specifically for using an AI voice. — [search across Variety, Music Ally, DMN, Podcastify; see sources above]

**Spotify's own AI voice products (context)**
- Spotify's AI-generated "personal podcasts" feature drew "intense backlash"; Spotify's Ringer network cancelled a ChatGPT partnership after fan backlash ("our audience just didn't like them" — Bill Simmons). — [Yahoo Tech (syndicated)](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/spotify-receives-intense-backlash-launching-141500971.html) (403 on direct fetch; summary from search); [Quartz](https://qz.com/ringer-chatgpt-partnership-canceled-fan-backlash-111326)

### Inferences
- The regulatory risk for AI-narrated original storytelling on Spotify is currently low on paper: the enforceable lines are impersonation, deception, bot/artificial audience and spam patterns (mass uploads, duplicate feeds). A network that mass-launches many near-identical AI feeds would look like the "one firm, 10,000 shows" pattern Spotify and Podcast Index are now tracking.
- Spotify's music-side trajectory (AI Personas excluded from recommendations by default; AI Credits) is a plausible template for podcasts, which Spotify explicitly called "the first in a series of steps"; a future disclosure/label regime for AI podcasts should be treated as likely but undocumented.
- Lacking a "Verified by Spotify" badge (which requires authenticated creator identity and sustained activity) may become a discovery disadvantage for faceless AI shows.

### Gaps
- Spotify's Podcast Monetization Policies and Platform Rules text could not be fetched; whether they contain "synthetic media" language is unverified.
- No official Spotify statement on whether AI-narrated podcasts are eligible for Premium video revenue or Verified badges.
- The Variety article (paywalled via Tollbit) was not read directly.

## Key Question 3: Sleep stories, bedtime stories, "boring history", audio drama and fiction on Spotify — audience, charts, monetization models

### Takeaway
The best-documented sleep show, Nothing Much Happens, monetizes mainly through a ~$40/year ad-free membership (13–14k members, ~$520k/year, 97–98% renewal) plus pre-roll-only ads, books, app and merch; Get Sleepy runs ad-supported free feed plus $5.99/month premium; Sleep With Me (2.3M monthly downloads in 2017) publicly reported in Sept 2025 that its premium subscription was failing and the show was financially unsustainable. Fiction/drama is a stable ~16–17% of monthly podcast listeners (Sounds Profitable), with large audio dramas relying on Patreon rather than ads.

### Cited Findings
- Nothing Much Happens (Kathryn Nicolai): started Apr 2018; 10M downloads within ~a year with no paid marketing; 150M+ downloads total; paid tier $40/year or $5/month gives ad-free feeds across three shows plus bonus/long-form (up to 10-hour) tracks; 13,000–14,000 members; ~$520k/year from membership; 97–98% annual renewal; subscriptions launched 2021–22 via Supporting Cast, Apple Podcasts subscriptions added later; ads since 2020, pre-roll only (mid-roll refused), which shrank the advertiser pool and pushed her to a boutique network; also book deal (sold in 35 countries), Simon & Schuster audio original, app, merch. Discovery came from Apple's New & Noteworthy. She does not use AI or freelancers for writing. — [IBTimes UK, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/kathryn-nicolai-sleep-podcast-success-1807274); [Side Hustle Nation interview](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/my-intentionally-boring-podcast-turned-into-a-media-empire/)
- Nothing Much Happens third-party ad-market listing: ~196.8k audience, 88% host-read ads (aggregator estimate, unconfirmed). — [Spotsnow](https://spotsnow.io/show/nothing-much-happens-bedtime-stories-to-help-you-sleep)
- Get Sleepy (Slumber Studios): free ad-supported feed; premium $5.99/month or $4.17/month annual via Supercast; past advertisers include Shopify, Rocket Money, BetterHelp, Helix Sleep. No revenue figures public. — [Get Sleepy Premium](https://get-sleepy.supercast.com/); [Libsyn advertising page](https://advertising.libsyn.com/GetSleepySleepmeditationandstories)
- Sleep With Me (Drew Ackerman): 2.3M monthly downloads in Jan 2017; joined Night Vale Presents Mar 2018; in 2025 the show "has been struggling financially" and was "largely unsuccessful" in attracting enough premium subscribers, with Ackerman saying he would need to step away (episode #1248, 15 Sep 2025). — [Wikipedia: Sleep with Me (podcast)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_with_Me_(podcast)); undated Podbean note of "almost 4,000 patrons" on Patreon — [Podbean](https://www.podbean.com/ew/dir-m7ghx-3f05092)
- Fiction/drama listening: 16% of monthly podcast listeners consumed drama/fiction in the past 30 days (down from 17%), while single-genre fiction listeners grew on Spotify. — [Sounds Profitable: The One-Show Wonders](https://soundsprofitable.com/article/the-one-show-wonders/)
- Audio-drama monetization: ad revenue works mainly for large audiences; Acast and Spotify both offer dynamic-ad programs for fiction; The Magnus Archives and Welcome to Night Vale cited as Patreon-funded shows earning substantial monthly income. — [ArtsHub](https://www.artshub.co.uk/news/features/the-rise-of-diy-audio-dramas-is-podcast-fiction-the-new-fringe-theatre-2624445/)
- Nicolai said that when she started in 2018, Sleep With Me was the only other sleep podcast and "the category now has hundreds." — [IBTimes UK, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/kathryn-nicolai-sleep-podcast-success-1807274)

### Inferences
- Sleep/bedtime content is unusually subscription-friendly (ad-free is the product) but ad-hostile (mid-rolls wake listeners), so the SPP's requirement to insert at least one ad break to qualify for Premium video revenue conflicts with the genre's norms; pre-roll-only placement is the workable compromise Nicolai uses.
- Sleep listening generates very high hours-per-listener (episodes played to the end, often looped), which maps well onto SPP's consumption-hour thresholds and Premium video formula, but the "video" requirement is awkward for an audio-first bedtime use case.
- The Sleep With Me case shows that even a top-50 sleep show with millions of downloads can fail to convert to subscriptions; success is concentrated in a few brands with strong authorial identity.

### Gaps
- No current Spotify chart positions or Spotify-specific audience figures for sleep/fiction categories were found.
- No data on Calm's Sleep Stories distribution on Spotify or any Calm podcast monetization.
- No documented AI-narrated sleep/fiction show with disclosed revenue.

## Key Question 4: Apple Podcasts, Amazon/Audible (Virtual Voice), YouTube, and hosting/ad networks — thresholds, AI rules, CPMs

### Takeaway
Apple Podcasts Subscriptions pay 70% (85% after a subscriber's first year) for a $19.99/year program fee with no audience threshold; Audible/KDP Virtual Voice pays a flat 40% royalty on AI-narrated audiobooks (invite-only beta, US, $3.99–$14.99 price band, Audible Plus paid from a separate fund) while standard ACX still requires human narration; YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" update demonetizes mass-produced, repetitive AI voiceover content but allows AI as a tool. Ad networks begin at ~1,000 monthly listeners (marketplaces) to 5,000+ downloads/episode (host-read), with 2026 CPMs of $15–$30 programmatic and $25–$50 host-read.

### Cited Findings

**Apple Podcasts**
- Apple Podcasters Program: creator receives 70% of subscription price per billing cycle minus taxes; rises to 85% after a subscriber accumulates one year of paid service; US fee $19.99/year; other revenue (ads) stays 100% with creator. — [Apple Podcasts for Creators: Program overview](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/apple-podcasters-program-overview)
- Apple reports podcast subscription revenue inside Services and does not break it out. — [usedots.com (secondary)](https://usedots.com/blog/how-podcast-platforms-pay-hosts/)
- No Apple Podcasts policy specific to AI-generated podcast content was found in this pass. — [search summary]

**Amazon/Audible**
- KDP "audiobooks with virtual voice": invite-only beta for eligible KDP eBooks in the US marketplace; 40% royalty on sales on Amazon and Audible ("in the future, we may pay at a lower royalty rate for books sold below the minimum list price"); KDP Select ebooks auto-enroll the audiobook; Audible Plus listening is paid from a separate fund. — [KDP Help: Virtual Voice](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GHJW2N8GLTQLK9TY); [KDP Help: eligibility](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GWHSFAK69P2537YR); [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/audio-books/article/93625-kindle-direct-publishing-will-beta-test-virtual-voice-narrated-audiobooks.html)

## Key Question 6: Risks — video dependence, discovery for new shows, payout changes

### Takeaway
SPP's Premium revenue is video-only and requires ad breaks, the "proprietary payment formula" can be changed unilaterally and eligibility "may change at any time"; no payout cut was reported through Nov 2026 (Spotify reports payouts up a third since Jan 2026), but the formula is a pooled, consumption-weighted share, so per-hour value falls as more shows and markets join unless Premium revenue grows proportionally. New faceless shows face a growing AI-feed glut (≈35% of new feeds in May 2026), verification badges favoring authenticated creators, and the hours threshold before any payout.

### Cited Findings
- Premium video revenue requires video episodes with at least one ad break; audio-only episodes earn only the ad share; the formula is "proprietary" and Spotify "may adjust" it; eligibility criteria "may change at any time." — [Spotify Support: Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/)
- Factor: "overall eligible video content consumed by Premium users in these markets" — i.e., a pooled share. — [Spotify Support: Spotify Partner Program](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program/)
- Ads are not guaranteed; they play only when advertiser demand exists for the show. — [Spotify Support: Partner Program analytics](https://support.spotify.com/us/creators/article/spotify-partner-program-analytics/)
- No reports of a payout cut found; Spotify says monthly payouts rose by a third since Jan 2026 and participating shows' consumption rose 45% on average. — [TechCrunch, 17 Sep 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/spotify-expands-its-partner-program-for-podcasts-to-35-new-countries/)
- Sleep show Nothing Much Happens: ad revenue is "seasonal," subscriptions the most reliable income. — [IBTimes UK, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/kathryn-nicolai-sleep-podcast-success-1807274)
- Spotify says US "superfans" stream nearly 20x more of a show than casual listeners and are 2.5x more likely to stay after six months, which is the basis of its "engagement" framing. — [TheWrap, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/business/spotify-partner-program-podcasts-revenue-creators/); [Spotify Newsroom, 7 Jan 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-01-07/spotify-partner-program-updates)
- Verified badge criteria (sustained activity, good standing, audience authenticity) and Spotify's statement that updates are "the first in a series of steps." — [Music Business Worldwide, 20 May 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-extends-verified-by-spotify-badges-to-podcasts-further-cracking-down-on-ai-impersonators/)
- A Medium essay (Aug 2026) argues a daily AI show can spend more on narration than it earns because the 2,000-hour wall precedes the first cent, citing a first payout of $48/month; the page could not be fetched (403) and the figures are unverified. — [Medium (unverified)](https://medium.com/@automation.labs/ai-makes-a-podcast-in-minutes-spotifys-first-payout-is-48-a-month-72712b0acabb)
- On the music side Spotify already excludes AI Personas from recommendations by default — a precedent for discovery penalties on disclosed-AI content. — [Spotify Newsroom, 20 Oct 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-10-20/prioritizing-human-artistry/)

### Inferences
- An AI-narrated story network's SPP economics depend on (1) producing video versions (even static/visualizer video) with an ad break, (2) concentrating audience in Premium-heavy markets, and (3) clearing the hour threshold per feed; a diversified stack (SPP + Apple/Spotify Memberships + Virtual Voice audiobooks) hedges against formula changes.
- Spotify's incentive to keep eligibility low (consumption +45% for participants) suggests near-term rates are stable, but the pooled formula and the 2026 influx of 35 markets and AI feeds create medium-term dilution risk that Spotify's aggregate "+33% payouts" figure cannot rule out at the per-show level.

### Gaps
- No public data on per-show payout trends (dilution) after the Oct 2026 expansion.
- No official statement on whether static-image/visualizer "video" qualifies as a video episode for Premium video revenue.
- Spotify's Partner Program Terms (contract text) were not retrievable to confirm termination/clawback provisions.
