## Key Question 1: Case studies with revenue / view / team data

### Takeaway
The only channels with hard, creator-disclosed economics are Kurzgesagt (YouTube ads = just 13% of revenue; 60+ staff) and Fall of Civilizations (Patreon ~$9K–$28K/month est.). Everything else is third-party estimation. The consistent pattern: top narrative-education channels run on diversified income (merch, Patreon, Nebula, sponsors), and ad revenue alone is a minority of revenue for the biggest players.

### Cited Findings

**Kurzgesagt (science, animated, ~20M subs) — creator disclosed [verified, 2015–2022 data]**
- Revenue mix over 8 years: online shop/merch 40%, YouTube ads 13%, commercial sponsors 12%, institutional sponsors/grants 10% (Gates-linked ~3%, Open Philanthropy ~5%, others ~2%), Patreon 9%, paid agency work 9% (stopped 2022), German public broadcaster ~7% (ended 2022). Audience directly/indirectly provides 62% of revenue. — [Kurzgesagt "How We Make Money on YouTube with 20M Subs" (transcript summary)](https://opentools.ai/youtube-summary/how-we-make-money-on-youtube-with-20m-subs)
- Team: "over 60 full-time employees in 2023, plus freelancers worldwide"; salaries alone cost "millions of dollars a year." First profit came in 2015. Cost per video and hours per video were not disclosed. — [same source](https://opentools.ai/youtube-summary/how-we-make-money-on-youtube-with-20m-subs)
- Third-party revenue/headcount estimates conflict wildly: Latka $8.5M (2025), Growjo $21.9M/yr, Influencer Fee $10–15M, Networth Spot ~$1.2M/yr (ads only), Owler <$1M; headcount estimates 70–90. Treat all as [estimate]. — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/kurzgesagt.org); [Growjo](https://growjo.com/company/in_a_nutshell_%E2%80%93_kurzgesagt); [Influencer Fee](https://influencerfee.com/influencer/kurzgesagt/); [Networth Spot](https://www.networthspot.com/kurzgesagt-in-a-nutshell/net-worth/); [Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/kurzgesagt)

### Inferences
- Kurzgesagt's 13% ad share shows that at the very top, a narrative-education brand becomes a merch/membership business with YouTube as the funnel; a new portfolio should model ad revenue as the floor, not the business.
- Fall of Civilizations demonstrates that a one-person, slow-cadence (a few episodes per year), extremely high-quality history brand can sustain ~$10K–$28K/month from ~3,800 patrons alone, independent of RPM — depth beats cadence for membership revenue.
- The Whistler model (one presenter, outsourced writing/editing, ~10 channels) is the proven template for a portfolio, but it relies on a recognisable human host/voice — which also happens to be the strongest defence under the inauthentic-content rules (see Q3).
- AI sleep-history channels can reach ~$170K in 8 months, but the same case shows that revenue is fragile: templated thumbnails/titles/slideshow visuals were enough to trigger channel-level demonetization.

### Gaps
- No reliable team size, cost per video, or RPM disclosures found for OverSimplified, History Matters, LEMMiNO, Voices of the Past, Fern, RealLifeLore, Johnny Harris, Mustard, Invicta, Extra History, Simple History, Fire of Learning, or Kings and Generals (current).
- Social Blade earnings pages are partly login-gated; no Social Blade earnings ranges were retrieved for the named channels.
- The Whistler network's current channel list, hosts, and writer-pay structure were not retrieved (the /channels/ and /credits/ pages were not fetched).

## Key Question 2: Education RPM/CPM benchmarks 2025–2026 by sub-niche and geography

### Takeaway
The only measured (not guessed) 2026 dataset — AIR Media-Tech, 300 channels, 3,595 monetized channel-months, May 2025–May 2026 — puts Education & Science at a median long-form RPM of **$10.22** (P25 $2.31, P75 $19.50), ~4.4x the all-niche median of $2.30, with the highest ad-fill rate of any niche (77%). "Finance pays $20–30 RPM" claims come from advertiser CPM estimates, not channel payouts; AIR's measured finance median is $2.01 on a 3-channel sample.

### Cited Findings
- Methodology: "The figures here come from the real YouTube Analytics of 300 channels AIR Media-Tech works with"; "3,595 monetized channel-months between May 2025 and May 2026"; "Every number is a median, so no single viral channel can skew a niche." Niches with <10 channels are flagged as directional. [measured sample] — [AIR Media-Tech niche RPM/CPM 2026](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)
- Median long-form RPM by niche (P25–P75): Education & Science $10.22 ($2.31–$19.50); Transport* $5.69 ($3.17–$9.81); Lifestyle $2.98; News & Politics* $2.60 ($1.79–$3.62); Entertainment $2.43 ($0.88–$5.02, n=40); Gadgets & Tech $2.33; Music $2.28 (n=45); Gaming $2.05; Business & Finance* $2.01 ($0.73–$5.17, n=3); Health & Sport $1.23; Kids & Teens $0.33. (*fewer than 10 channels) — [AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)
- Education & Science: 77% of views serve an ad (highest), 1.84 ads per monetized view, top quartile ≈8.4x bottom quartile; Studio CPM $5.91; ~98,000 views per $1,000. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)
- Business & Finance: Q4 seasonal lift ~+60% (largest); Entertainment Q4 lift only ~+4%. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)
- Shorts RPM: $0.02–$1.48 by niche, roughly 3–14% of the same niche's long-form RPM (274 channels). — [AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)
- Education livestreams earn $17.58 per 1,000 views, highest live rate of any niche. — [AIR livestream data](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/livestream-rpm-revenue-and-subscribers-by-youtube-niche-the-2025-numbers)
- Geography: AIR's country table (June 2025, CPM, >3,000 channels, all niches) lists US $14.67 CPM vs India $0.74; no education-specific country breakdown exists. — [AIR 1M views calculator](https://air.io/en/monetization/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1-million-views); [Mubert summary](https://mubert.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche-and-country)
- Estimate-based lists diverge: finance $10–$30 (ytmoneycalculator) or $15–$50 (FluxNote); education $4–$12 (FluxNote); a history-channel guide claims "$6–14 RPM." [estimate, vendor blogs] — [ytmoneycalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/average-youtube-rpm/); [FluxNote niche ranking](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-niche-profitability-ranking-2026); [FluxNote history guide](https://fluxnote.io/blog/history-youtube-channel-guide-2026)
- One analysis notes lists that put finance at $20–30 "usually quote advertiser estimates, not what channels are paid." — [clipflux](https://clipflux.ai/guides/youtube-rpm-by-niche)
- RPM includes non-ad revenue (memberships, Super Chat) and not all views carry ads. — [YouTube Help: ad revenue analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357)

### Inferences
- For planning, use **$10 RPM as the median case, $2.3 as the bear case, $19.5 as the bull case** for English-language history/science long-form. The 8.4x intra-niche spread is driven mostly by audience geography (US/UK/DE/AU vs global) and viewer age, so a channel targeting US/UK adults 35+ should sit above median; non-English or youth-skewed channels should plan near the bottom quartile.
- True-crime and geopolitics have no separate measured line; News & Politics ($2.60) suggests geopolitics content classified as "news" earns far below education, so framing/metadata as educational explainer matters for RPM.
- At median RPM, 1M long-form views ≈ $10,220; at P25 ≈ $2,310; at P75 ≈ $19,500. [my arithmetic]

### Gaps
- No education-specific RPM by country or language found (AIR's country table is all-niche CPM).
- No measured 2025–2026 sub-niche splits for history vs science vs true crime vs geopolitics; all sit inside "Education & Science."
- No individual history/science creator RPM disclosure for 2025–2026 was found in accessible sources.

## Key Question 3: YouTube's inauthentic-content policy and AI-assisted educational narration

### Takeaway
YouTube's policy text explicitly permits AI as a tool ("the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision") and bars "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production," "templated storylines," and "readings of other materials you did not originally create." Enforcement in 2025–2026 (16 channels, 35M subs, 4.7B views wiped in Jan 2026; a $170K sleep-history channel demonetized) targeted fully automated pipelines with identical thumbnails/titles/slideshows — not AI voice per se.

### Cited Findings
**Official policy text [verified, YouTube Help "YouTube channel monetization policies"]**
- Changelog, July 15, 2025: "We're making a minor update to our 'repetitious content' policy to better clarify this includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced"; "renaming this policy from 'repetitious content' to 'inauthentic content'"; "This type of content has always been ineligible for monetization under our existing policies"; "There is no change to our reused content policy." — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Not allowed (Generic or Repetitive): "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation across videos"; "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production." Allowed: "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different." — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- AI/creative-vision language (Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content): "If you use automated tools or templates to help create your content, the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision." Allowed examples: "using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented"; "using AI to edit your video scripts or generate a unique background visual." — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Narration rules: not allowed — "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create"; "Content that gets views from mostly non-verbal reactions to your videos without added voice commentary." Reused: not allowed — "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications." — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Note: the live Help page still titles the section "Generic or Repetitive Content"; "inauthentic content" appears only in the changelog. — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)

**July 2026 clarification (three categories) [secondary press; could not be verified against the Help page]**
- Tubefilter (July 13, 2026) reported YouTube clarified three ineligible types: (1) generic/repetitive template content, (2) unsatisfying/off-putting content (emotionally manipulative formulas, interchangeable format copies, shock content), (3) "AI personas delivering information on sensitive topics, such as health, legal, finance, or politics" (e.g., an AI 'doctor'). — [OutlierKit summary citing Tubefilter](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/); [Viddar](https://viddar.io/en/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-monetization); [Faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/)
- eWeek reports YouTube trust & safety head Matt Halprin appeared in a Creator Insider video to clarify "inauthentic content," barring generic AI videos, distressing clips, and AI personas discussing health or finance. — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos)
- Rene Ritchie (Head of Creator Liaison) is quoted as saying YouTube "welcomes creators using AI tools to enhance storytelling"; channels using AI "remain eligible for monetization." — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- Neal Mohan's January 2026 annual letter used the term "AI slop" and named managing it a top 2026 priority (per CNBC, Jan 21, 2026). — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/)

**Enforcement cases**
- Jan 2026: YouTube terminated 11 channels and wiped 6 others (16 total): ~35M subscribers, ~4.7B lifetime views, est. $9.8M/yr ad revenue (Kapwing analysis). Largest: CuentosFacianantes (5.95M, AI Dragon Ball narratives), Imperiodejesus (5.87M, AI-narrated biblical stories, multiple uploads daily), Super Cat League (4.21M), Three Minute Wisdom (1.7M subs, 2B views; content wiped). YouTube spokesperson: removals were for violating existing spam and deceptive-practices policies — not a new AI rule. Common pattern: AI handled scripting, voiceover, visuals and publishing with no human editorial input. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/youtube-cracks-down-ai-generated-content-1800213); [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2589980/youtube-cracks-down-deletes-47b-views-of-ai-spam-videos)
- Sleep-history case: AI-narrated, long-form "sleep-friendly" history channel demonetized; signals identified: nearly identical thumbnails, one repeated title structure, long slideshow-style videos with slow generated visuals; ~$170K ad revenue May–Dec 2025 before demonetization. Author's reading (not YouTube's statement) is that the combination, not AI voice alone, triggered it. — [TubeBuddy](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-ai-demonetization-how-to-spot-risk/)
- A July 2026 creator video reports "several fast-growing AI History channels" being demonetized. — [YouTube: "AI HISTORY Channels Are Getting Demonetized!"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K7aYHL0yLOE)
- Scalelab (citing AIR Media-Tech) says "long sleep and prayer compilations lost monetization." [secondary] — [Scalelab](https://scalelab.com/en/why-youtube-channel-demonetized-10-reasons-and-how-to-prevent-them)
- Cautionary precedent: The Armchair Historian (human-made, animated) also had repeated demonetization problems around war/history topics and paused production in 2019 — history content carries advertiser-suitability risk independent of AI. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Armchair_Historian)

**What distinguishes compliant AI-assisted channels (industry guidance, secondary)**
- Lower risk: AI tools enhancing genuinely original content; AI-assisted editing with human creative direction; AI research/scripting help with human judgment. Higher risk: AI slideshows with no real narration/editing; AI-only voiceovers on stock footage; AI scripting+voice+publishing with no editorial review. "Key test: Can a viewer tell that a human made creative decisions?" — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/)
- Consistency is acceptable (branding, repeatable formats, intros) "as long as each upload offers a different angle, insight, story, or payoff"; risk behaviours: full automation, script recycling, upload flooding, mass templates. — [TubeBuddy](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-ai-demonetization-how-to-spot-risk/)
- Using an AI voice for narration is a production choice and does not by itself require disclosure; disclosure is required for "meaningfully altered or synthetically generated" content that could be mistaken for reality (realistic depictions). — [Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/youtube-ai-content-policy); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/)
- Creators criticise the criteria as vague/non-transparent. — [SubSub](https://www.subsub.io/blog/inauthentic-content-why-so-many-creators-are-getting-demonetized)

### Inferences
- The policy's safe harbour for a narrative-education portfolio is: original researched scripts (not readings of Wikipedia/public-domain texts), a recognisable editorial voice, bespoke visuals per video (maps, animations, archival), varied titles/thumbnails, and a cadence that cannot be mistaken for flooding. AI voice is tolerated but is the single most-cited signal in demonetized cases, so a human narrator (or at least a cloned voice of a real, on-record host) materially lowers portfolio risk.
- Multi-channel portfolios are specifically exposed: the same template replicated across 10 channels is literally the "interchangeable format copies" category. Each channel needs a distinct visual/editorial identity.
- The third July-2026 category (AI personas on health/legal/finance/politics) means AI-hosted geopolitics or finance-history explainers are higher risk than AI-narrated ancient history or science.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the July 2026 "three categories" language or the Matt Halprin Creator Insider video against YouTube's own Help page or Creator Insider channel (only press summaries retrieved).
- No published YouTube data on what share of appeals succeed, or on how AI-voice-with-original-script channels fare statistically.
- The $21K/month "remonetized AI sleep channel" claim was not verified beyond the video title.

## Key Question 4: Time to monetization, growth curves, and the Feb 1, 2027 YPP change

### Takeaway
From February 1, 2027, new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 qualified watch hours (365 days) or 20M Shorts views (90 days) — double today's 4,000 hours; existing partners are grandfathered and applications received by 23:59 UTC Jan 31, 2027 are judged at the old bar. No verified history-channel "0 to 100K" case study with timelines was found; vendor anecdotes put YPP eligibility at 4–12 months and early AdSense at $50–150/month.

### Cited Findings
- New bar from Feb 1, 2027: 1,000 subscribers + "8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days"; subscriber requirement unchanged; fan-funding tier unchanged at 500 subs + 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views. Earning from Shorts requires 10M qualified Shorts views in trailing 90 days, checked monthly. Sources cited: YouTube Help "Changes to the YouTube Partner Program" (answer/12843009) and a YouTube Blog post. — [AIR Media-Tech YPP 2026 guide](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)
- Grandfathering: channels already earning keep YPP status; existing partners must accept updated terms in Studio by 31 January 2027; an application reaching YouTube by 23:59 UTC Jan 31 is judged at the 4,000-hour threshold even if decided later (attributed to AIR's partner support, not YouTube). Review takes ~a month; submitting early December leaves room for one rejection + 30-day wait + retry. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)
- New inactivity rule from Feb 2027: "active" = 1,000 watch hours/365 days, or 1M Shorts views/90 days, or two long-form videos or five Shorts every 90 days, with a 90-day recovery window. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)
- Announcement dated by third parties to Aug 10–11, 2026; not retroactive. — [SocialDay](https://socialday.live/features/youtube-raises-partner-program-entry-bar-to-8-000-watch-hours-from-february-2027); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- Current (pre-Feb-2027) thresholds: Tier 1 fan funding 500 subs + 3,000 hours/12 mo or 3M Shorts/90 d + 3 public uploads/90 d; Tier 2 full monetization 1,000 subs + 4,000 hours or 10M Shorts views. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)
- Vendor guidance [estimate]: "most history channels take 4–12 months" to YPP eligibility; "expect $50–$150/month in AdSense initially"; anecdotes of a dark-history channel at 31K subs in 5 months and another monetized at 9,400 subs in month 3 (unverified marketing pages). — [FluxNote history guide](https://fluxnote.io/blog/history-youtube-channel-guide-2026); [FluxNote case](https://fluxnote.io/guides/divya-s-dark-history-channel-journey)
- A maths-channel creator: hitting goals "may take 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, or more than 3 years." — [Medium](https://medium.com/new-writers-welcome/7-lessons-after-publishing-100-videos-on-a-maths-youtube-channel-22083ad88564)
- Off-niche fast examples (vendor marketing): sports channel monetized in 40 days with $6,563.78 ad revenue over 60 days (OpusClip); a new channel earning $1,200 AdSense in month one (Techyrack). — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/from-zero-to-monetization-for-a-new-youtube-channel-in-40-days); [Techyrack](https://www.techyrack.com/2025/08/i-took-brand-new-youtube-channel-from-0.html)

### Inferences
- Watch-hour math [my arithmetic]: for 15–20 min videos averaging ~6–8 minutes watched, 4,000 hours ≈ 30,000–40,000 views; 8,000 hours ≈ 60,000–80,000 views within 365 days. The doubling roughly doubles the pre-revenue runway for each new channel in a portfolio, which compounds across 10 channels.
- Launch-plan implication: any channel in the portfolio that can plausibly hit 1,000 subs + 4,000 hours by mid-December 2026 should apply before the cutoff; channels launched after ~October 2026 will almost certainly face the 8,000-hour bar.
- Long-form education is favourably placed vs Shorts-first channels: 8,000 hours of 15-minute documentary views is far more attainable than 20M Shorts views, and sleep/background formats accumulate watch hours fast — but see Q3 for the policy risk of that format.
- The new inactivity rule (2 long-form uploads per 90 days) is a low bar for a documentary channel but must be tracked across a portfolio so dormant channels don't lose YPP.

### Gaps
- No verified, history/science-specific 2024–2026 growth curve (0→1K→100K subs with dates and revenue) was found; r/PartneredYoutube and r/NewTubers were not accessible in search results.

## Key Question 5: Production cost — human vs AI-assisted pipeline for a 15–20 min documentary video

### Takeaway
Published freelance rates imply a fully human 15–20 min documentary-style video costs roughly $1,500–$6,000+ (script $300–$3,000, narration $150–$1,000, editing $300–$1,500+), while AI voice costs cents to a few dollars per video; the human cost that remains in AI-assisted pipelines is editorial judgment (research, script review, visual direction, editing QA), which is also what the monetization policy demands.

## Key Question 7: Audience demand signals (long-form education, TV-screen viewing, sleep/background viewing)

### Takeaway
YouTube hit a record 15.8% of all US TV-screen viewing in August 2026 (Nielsen Gauge; Netflix 7.7%), with 50+ viewership up 9% — a demographic and device shift that favours long-form documentary formats. No platform-level statistic specific to educational watch time was found; demand for sleep/background history is evidenced only by channel growth and widespread commentary on AI saturation.

### Cited Findings
- Nielsen The Gauge, Aug 2026: YouTube 15.8% of US TV watch time (its highest ever), Netflix 7.7% (second); streaming nearly half of US TV-screen traffic; total TV usage down 4.4% July→Aug; YouTube viewership among 50+ up 9%; gains attributed partly to older viewers and sports rights (NFL Sunday Ticket). — [Tubefilter, Oct 22, 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/10/22/nielsen-the-gauge-youtube-record-watch-time-share-us-tv/)
- July 2026: YouTube 14.2% of US TV time; streaming overall 49.0% (Nielsen via Digital Applied). — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/youtube-statistics-2026-platform-data-points)
- Claims that >50% of US YouTube watch time is on TV screens are secondary (YouTube publishes no device share). — [Kidelight](https://www.kidelight.com/2026/10/why-50-of-youtube-watch-time-is-on-your.html); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/youtube-statistics-2026-platform-data-points)

- Sleep-history format: Sleepless Historian ~702K subs; Sleep Theories 71.7K subs/7.92M views (Jul 2026) adding 100K+ views weekly; History Before Sleep 55K subs/5.8M views; commentary describes "an infinite number of these boring history videos for sleep" and channels that specialise in nothing but AI-generated 'boring' videos. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/sleeplesshistorian); [SocialCounts](https://socialcounts.org/youtube-channel-analytics/UCVmOv-b-SDTEZOKsMaTpLBQ); [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/historybeforesleep88); [Morgan Gold Substack](https://morgangold.substack.com/p/i-cant-fall-asleep-without-robots); [Professional Moron](https://professionalmoron.com/2026/02/07/the-sleepy-world-of-boring-history-ai-videos/)

### Inferences
- The TV-screen + 50+ demographic shift is the strongest macro tailwind for 15–45 minute narrative history/science: it looks like linear documentary TV, which is what that audience historically watched, and TV-screen views generally carry higher CPMs (consistent with Education's $10+ RPM).
- Sleep/background history is a demonstrably large demand pool but is saturated with AI output, has an unproven ad-engagement profile, and is the exact format in the demonetization case studies — it is the highest-risk sub-format for a portfolio despite fast watch-hour accumulation.

- No measured data on whether sleep/background viewing has lower RPM or fill rate than active educational viewing.

## Illustrative single-channel P&L frame (my arithmetic from the cited benchmarks; not a sourced figure)

- Revenue per 1M long-form views: bear $2,310 (P25) / base $10,220 (median) / bull $19,500 (P75) — Education & Science, AIR 2026.
- Pre-monetization runway: 4,000 watch hours (≈30–40K views of 15–20 min videos) if admitted before Feb 1, 2027; 8,000 hours (≈60–80K views) after; vendor anecdotes suggest 4–12 months for history channels.
- Cost per 15–20 min video: AI-assisted with human editorial $150–$950; hybrid (human narrator + editor) $900–$2,500; fully human freelance $1,900–$5,500.
- Breakeven views/video at base RPM: ~15K–95K (AI-assisted), ~90K–245K (hybrid), ~185K–540K (fully human). At bear RPM, multiply by ~4.4.
- Upside levers evidenced by case studies: Patreon (Fall of Civilizations ~$9K–$28K/mo on ~3,800 patrons), merch (Kurzgesagt 40% of revenue), Nebula (50% profit pool, invite-only), sponsorships (Education's high CPM/fill suggests strong brand demand).
- Key risk: channel-level demonetization under inauthentic-content rules for templated multi-channel AI output; mitigants are original scripts, distinct visual identities per channel, varied packaging, and a human (or on-record) narrator.
