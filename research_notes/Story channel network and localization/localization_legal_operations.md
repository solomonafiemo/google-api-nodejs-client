# Legal, Contractual and Operational Structure for a YouTube Creator-Localization (Dubbing) Company — state as of 14 November 2026

Scope note: this brief covers a company that (a) licenses YouTube creators' catalogs, (b) produces dubbed/localized versions, (c) publishes them on channels it owns or via the creator's multi-language audio (MLA) tracks, (d) collects AdSense revenue as payee and (e) pays the creator a share. Primary sources (Google/YouTube Help, IRS, statutes, law-firm alerts) are preferred; trade-press and vendor claims are flagged as such. Research was tool-limited (one EU AI Act law-firm page returned HTTP 403; Business Insider reporting on Jellysmack/Spotter could not be retrieved directly).

## Key Question 1 — Rights a localization license must cover (derivative works, name/likeness, channel branding, music, Content ID conflicts) and how Jellysmack/Spotter deals were reported to be structured

### Takeaway
A dubbed re-upload is a derivative work plus a re-publication of the original audiovisual asset, so the license must grant translation/adaptation/dubbing rights, reproduction and public-communication rights for each language channel, and separate name/likeness/branding and voice rights; third-party music licenses are typically safelisted per channel/video and do not automatically follow the content to a new channel. The two best-known catalog financiers (Spotter, Jellysmack) used fixed-term (about five-year) catalog licenses with upfront lump sums, creator retaining ownership — a useful template for term and structure, but their revenue-share percentages were never reliably published.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's own MLA documentation warns that a secondary audio track is separately scanned for copyright: "If we detect that the copyrighted content in the secondary audio track is different from the original audio" the file may be removed — i.e., music/sound in a dub is evaluated on its own — [YouTube Help, "Add Multi-language features to your videos"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784?hl=en)
- YouTube's monetization policy applies its reused-content rules "even if you have permission from the original creator," and explicitly lists "Promotion of other people's content (even if you have permission)" and "Collections of songs from different artists (even if you have their permission)" as not monetizable; "Reused content is separate from YouTube's Copyright enforcement" — [YouTube Help, YPP channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Epidemic Sound licenses are implemented as per-asset "safelisting" entries: a safelisting entry "protects a specific asset (channel or video) from copyright claims on a given platform," so a new language channel is not covered unless it is itself safelisted/linked — [Epidemic Sound developer docs, Safelisting](https://developers.epidemicsound.com/docs/safelisting/)
- Epidemic Sound's help center says a Content ID claim can arise when the channel is not correctly connected to the subscription or not correctly linked to an MCN in its system, and advises disputing within 5 days to avoid losing ad revenue; on long-form videos a claim is a monetization claim (revenue to Epidemic Sound), not a takedown — [Epidemic Sound Help, "Why was a Content ID claim received"](https://help.epidemicsound.com/hc/en-us/articles/26253712691730-Why-was-a-Content-ID-claim-received-from-Epidemic-Sound); [Epidemic Sound Help, MCN copyright inquiries](https://help.epidemicsound.com/hc/en-us/articles/26254699623826-MCN-Copyright-Inquiries)
- Once a channel is linked to an MCN/Content Manager, "the MCN's content owner can claim videos and apply monetization policies to that channel"; channel-level permissions are "Monetize uploads (non-affiliate channels only)," "View revenue," and "Set match policy" — [YouTube Help, MCN operations manual: Set channel-level permissions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6351520?hl=en)
- Jellysmack (Jan. 25, 2022) launched a $500M program to license "accomplished" creators' back catalogs for up to five years; it collects YouTube ad revenue during the license; creators keep ownership of IP and brand; lump sums reported from $50,000 to $50,000,000 per transaction — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/01/25/jellysmack-500-million-licensing-old-youtube-videos/)
- Jellysmack's own launch language: creators "retain full ownership of their content and intellectual property" and keep revenue from newly published content — [Jellysmack blog](https://blog.jellysmack.com/?p=336)
- Spotter (Feb. 2022): creators taking upfront capital "have to be willing to license their content to Spotter for five years"; Spotter's stated target is recouping in about four years — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/spotter-invest-youtubers-back-catalog/)
- Spotter deal sizes reported at $50,000–$50M; Spotter said it had paid out $600M by Sept. 2022 — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2022/09/19/spotter-catalog-licensing-youtube-fund-600-million-billion/)
- Spotter frames the structure as a content license, not a loan or equity; Spotter "absorbs the loss if a catalog earns less than projected and captures any upside during the license period" — [Faster Than Normal profile of Spotter](https://fasterthannormal.co/businesses/spotter) (secondary)
- Jellysmack sold its JellyFi catalog-licensing unit to Copyright Capital; former employees told Business Insider Jellysmack's offers were often less lucrative than Spotter's — reported via [NetInfluencer summarizing Business Insider](https://www.netinfluencer.com/jellysmack-failed-jellyfi-500m-creator-buyout-plan/) (secondary; BI original not retrieved)
- An "80/20 split in the creator's favor" for Jellysmack's distribution business is asserted only by a PR-style blog and should be treated as unverified — [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/jellysmack-the-creator-distribution-engine-operating-at-scale)
- Spotter–YouTube MLA partnership (Sept. 28, 2023): Spotter funded MrBeast's dubbing into 13 then 14 languages (German added) and converted part of Airrack's library into Spanish/Portuguese; creators "keep control of their catalogs, channels, and future earnings"; no revenue-share terms disclosed — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/spotter-and-youtube-partner-in-supporting-creator-expansion-to-global-audiences-301941766.html)
- Linguana (Tubefilter, Dec. 11, 2025) "built and manages" 13 localized channels for SLAV'S ADVENTURES (3.6M subs; >60M views on the dubbed channels), mirrors the creator's bio/links/metadata, charges no upfront fee and takes a revenue share (percentage undisclosed); uses AI voices "trained on each creator's own voice"; human voice acting cited at roughly $20–$50 per finished minute — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/11/linguana-creators-localized-channels-ai-clone-voices/) (Tubefilter discloses Linguana is a partner)

### Inferences
- The license should expressly grant, per language and territory: (i) the right to create derivative works (translation, adaptation, re-voicing, re-editing, new thumbnails/titles); (ii) reproduction, distribution and public communication of the original visuals on partner-owned channels and/or as audio tracks on the creator's channel; (iii) the right to register the dubbed versions in Content ID (and an obligation on the creator not to claim them); (iv) name, image, likeness, channel name/logo/branding and persona (trademark/trade-dress) rights to "mirror" the main channel; and (v) a separate, specific voice-replica consent (see Q2).
- Because YouTube scans the secondary audio track independently, and because stock-music licenses (e.g., Epidemic Sound) are safelisted per channel/video, the partner must either (a) obtain its own music licenses and safelist each language channel, (b) require the creator to add the language channels to the creator's music subscription, or (c) strip/replace music in the dub. The contract should allocate who bears Content ID monetization claims on the dubbed channels.
- The Jellysmack/Spotter template suggests a market-accepted structure of a 5-year fixed-term license, creator retaining ownership, financier collecting 100% of ad revenue on licensed assets during the term in exchange for upfront cash. For a dubbing company, the analogous structure is: no upfront fee + revenue share, or an advance recoupable against the creator's share.

### Gaps
- No published Jellysmack or Spotter contract text; revenue-share percentages (where any) are unverified. The Business Insider reporting was only available second-hand.
- No source found addressing whether creators' existing catalog-license deals (e.g., with Spotter) restrict their ability to grant dubbing rights to a third party — this must be checked by representation/warranty in the creator contract.

## Key Question 2 — Voice-cloning consent: statutory requirements (Tennessee ELVIS Act, California AB 2602/AB 1836, EU AI Act Article 50, NO FAKES Act status), vendor consent rules (ElevenLabs), and YouTube's synthetic-content disclosure and likeness detection

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 there is no enacted US federal digital-replica right (NO FAKES Act of 2026 cleared Senate Judiciary in June 2026 but has not passed either chamber); state law governs, with California AB 2602 making a replica clause unenforceable unless it describes intended uses with reasonable specificity or the creator was represented by counsel/union, and Tennessee's ELVIS Act creating liability for unauthorized voice use and for tools whose primary purpose is producing replicas. In the EU, Article 50 AI Act transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026, so a deployer publishing AI-dubbed video that makes a real person appear to say words they did not say should label it; YouTube itself treats "cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs" as not requiring disclosure, and ElevenLabs' Professional Voice Cloning only lets a person clone their own voice, which dictates the operational workflow.

### Cited Findings
- NO FAKES Act of 2026 reintroduced May 20, 2026 as S.4591 / H.R.8915 (sponsors Blackburn, Coons, Tillis, Klobuchar; Salazar, Dean, Moran, Balint); would create "a federal, licensable property right in their own voice and visual likeness"; adds a DMCA-style counter-notification with notarized signature and a 14-day restore window; $25,000-per-counter-notification liability for knowing misrepresentation; fourth version since a 2023 discussion draft — [Manatt, May 28, 2026](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/congress-reintroduces-the-no-fakes-act-what-s-new-in-the-2026-bill)
- Senate Judiciary Committee advanced the bill by voice vote in June 2026 (most sources say June 18, 2026; one says June 26); placed on the Senate calendar June 24, 2026; House companion not yet marked up; proposed statutory damages up to $750,000 per violation; revised text would preempt future state digital-replica laws — [Sen. Blackburn press release](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/index.php/2026/6/blackburn-coons-bipartisan-bill-to-protect-individuals-and-creators-from-deepfakes-passes-senate-judiciary-committee); [CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/gbr/publication/the-war-on-digital-replicas-the-no-fakes-act-advances); [Paubox](https://www.paubox.com/blog/senate-committee-advances-no-fakes-act)
- California AB 2602 (signed Sept. 17, 2024; effective Jan. 1, 2025; Labor Code § 927): a provision in a personal/professional services contract allowing a digital replica is enforceable only if it describes intended uses "with reasonable specificity," or the individual was represented by legal counsel who negotiated the license (with commercial terms signed or initialed) or by a union whose CBA addresses digital replicas; "digital replica" = computer-generated, highly realistic, readily identifiable representation — [Covington, Sept. 11, 2024](https://www.covingtonblogs.com/2024/09/11/california-passes-digital-replica-legislation-as-congress-considers-federal-approach/)
- California AB 1836 (effective Jan. 1, 2025; Civ. Code § 3344.1): prohibits nonconsensual production/distribution of digital replicas of deceased personalities' voice or likeness without estate consent — [Covington](https://www.covingtonblogs.com/2024/09/11/california-passes-digital-replica-legislation-as-congress-considers-federal-approach/)
- Tennessee ELVIS Act (enacted March 2024): adds "voice" to the state publicity statute, removes the advertising-only limit, imposes liability for knowingly distributing/making available unauthorized digital replicas, and separately for "algorithms, technologies, software, and tools" whose "primary purpose or function" is producing unauthorized replicas; gives standing to record labels/exclusive distributors — [Covington](https://www.covingtonblogs.com/2024/09/11/california-passes-digital-replica-legislation-as-congress-considers-federal-approach/); [Wilson Sonsini](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/the-elvis-act-setting-the-stage-for-policing-unauthorized-use-of-ai-generated-sound-and-likeness.html)
- EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026; the Digital Omnibus on AI gave providers of generative systems already on the market before that date until 2 December 2026 to meet the Article 50(2) machine-readable marking duty — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/?p=9009); [ICTRecht](https://www.ictrecht.nl/en/blog/heads-up-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-effect)
- Under Article 50(4) the deployer (not the provider) must disclose that deepfake content "has been artificially generated or manipulated"; "deep fake" (Art. 3(60)) = AI-generated/manipulated image, audio or video that resembles existing persons etc. and "would falsely appear authentic or truthful"; a machine-readable marker alone is insufficient; label must be clear, perceptible without technical aids, available at first exposure; T&Cs/URLs/hidden menus alone are insufficient; artistic/satirical works get an adapted-form label, not an exemption; Commission consultation draft guidelines published 8 May 2026 (consultation to 3 June 2026) — [SRD Rechtsanwälte](https://www.srd-rechtsanwaelte.de/en/blog/deepfake-labelling-under-the-ai-act-what-the-new-eu-guidance-clarifies); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nfqg/always-read-the-label-eu-ai-transparency-rules-and-questions-that-remain-unanswe/)
- Audio-industry commentary reads Art. 50(4) to cover a voice clone "used in place of something a real person never actually said," which would capture AI dubbing — [Production Expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/new-eu-ai-law-starts-monday-what-audio-pros-need-to-know) (secondary interpretation; one source says final Commission guidelines were published July 20, 2026 — [SecurePrivacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-for-chatbots-and-deepfakes-2026) — unverified against the Commission site)
- YouTube disclosure policy: creators must disclose realistic altered/synthetic content that "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do"; explicitly NOT requiring disclosure: "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs" (listed among minor/aesthetic edits); "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money"; YouTube may auto-label content detected as AI (C2PA metadata, its own tools, or detection) and labels applied after manual review "can not be adjusted"; persistent non-disclosure can lead to label application, content removal or YPP suspension — [YouTube Help, Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- Secondary reporting: label position moved below the player for long-form video and automatic AI labeling began around May 27, 2026 — [Minimatters](https://minimatters.com/?p=6160); [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/youtube-ai-labels-auto-detection) (secondary)
- YouTube likeness detection: expanded to all creators 18+ around May 16, 2026; works like Content ID "but scans for faces"; setup in YouTube Studio > Content detection > Likeness with selfie-video and government-ID verification; matches reviewed in a Likeness tab and removal requested via the privacy-complaint process; later extended to celebrities/talent agencies without a channel — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtubes-ai-face-shield-now-covers-every-creator-18-and-over); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/youtube-extends-deepfake-detection-tool-access-to-celebrities-and-talent-agencies/)
- Voice (speaking-voice) detection is announced but not deployed: YouTube said at Made on YouTube (Sept. 2026) it plans to add speaking-voice detection "later this year," combining audio and facial signals; Help page (answer 16440338) still describes facial matching only; nothing published on how licensed/authorized synthetic uses are handled — [ID Tech Wire, Sept. 25, 2026](https://idtechwire.com/youtube-plans-voice-detection-for-creator-likeness-checks/)
- ElevenLabs Professional Voice Cloning: "For now, we only allow you to clone your own voice... Even with their consent, you cannot clone someone else's voice"; every PVC requires voice verification (reading verification lines) before fine-tuning; to share a voice with another person, "they create and verify their own PVC, then share it with you through a sharing link"; Enterprise gets custom PVC slots; generated audio "can be traced back to the user who generated it" — [ElevenLabs docs, Professional Voice Cloning](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/product-guides/voices/voice-cloning/professional-voice-cloning)

### Inferences
- Operational consequence of ElevenLabs' rule: the creator (not the partner) must create and verify the PVC in the creator's own account and share it to the partner's account via a sharing link; the contract should obligate the creator to do this, keep the share active for the term, and permit revocation only on termination. Equivalent vendor checks (HeyGen etc.) should be assumed and verified.
- Draft the voice-replica clause to satisfy AB 2602 even for non-California creators (it is the strictest US contract-formation rule): enumerate uses (dubbing into listed languages, on listed channels/tracks, for the term), prohibit uses outside scope (ads, endorsements, new scripts not derived from the creator's own content), recommend/offer independent counsel review, and have the creator initial the replica terms.
- For EU-facing distribution, assume Art. 50(4) applies to AI-dubbed speech attributed to a real person: use YouTube's AI disclosure toggle (which YouTube does not require for self-voice dubs but which satisfies the "perceptible label" standard), plus a visible/audible notice in the description or an in-video card. Note the tension: YouTube treats self-voice dubs as disclosure-exempt, while EU law may still require labelling by the deployer.
- Because likeness detection (face now, voice soon) can flag the creator's own dubbed channels as matches, the contract should require the creator to "archive"/not file removal requests against authorized dubbed uploads and to coordinate before using the Likeness tab; the partner should keep written authorization ready for YouTube privacy-complaint reviews.
- Tennessee's tool-liability prong is a risk for the dubbing company only if its own pipeline's "primary purpose" is unauthorized replicas; documented consent for every voice mitigates this.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve HeyGen's consent/verification terms or ElevenLabs' commercial-rights terms (Terms of Service) directly; vendor commercial scope must be checked at contracting.
- Official EU Commission final Article 50 guidelines: status/date conflicting across secondary sources (draft 8 May 2026; one source says final 20 July 2026); the Travers Smith page returned HTTP 403.
- No official YouTube statement on how likeness detection will treat creator-authorized dubs.

## Key Question 3 — YouTube Content Manager (CMS) and MCN affiliation in 2026: eligibility, capabilities, affiliate terms, access for a small localization company, alternatives

### Takeaway
Content Manager remains partner-only, with MCN approval requiring that a company already "produce and monetize a significant amount of quality content on multiple channels" and be in YPP; a small localization company is unlikely to be granted CMS at the outset and should plan on owning the language channels directly (Brand Account/channel permissions with the partner as primary owner and AdSense payee) or working as a vendor to the creator via MLA tracks, moving to CMS/MCN status once scale justifies it.

### Cited Findings
- MCN eligibility guidelines: follow Terms of Service and Community Guidelines; "Produce and monetize a significant amount of quality content on multiple channels"; join YPP; consider YouTube Certified training; "These guidelines are subject to change without notice" — [YouTube Help, Basic MCN guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6361586?hl=en)
- "These features are only available to partners who use YouTube Studio Content Manager" — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6351520?hl=en)
- Within Content Manager, a content owner can set per-channel permissions: "Monetize uploads (non-affiliate channels only)," "View revenue" (channel owner sees Earnings Report), "Set match policy" (channel owner can enable Content ID matches and set match policies); an MCN "cannot disable an affiliate creator's ability to monetize their videos" because affiliates "are authorized to monetize eligible videos under YouTube Partner Program terms" — [YouTube Help, Set channel-level permissions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6351520?hl=en)
- Joining YPP "is a requirement for all affiliate channels in multi-channel networks (MCNs). Channels that are in an MCN and below the YPP threshold will be released from the MCN" — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7340882?hl=en)
- For affiliates, "Channel revenue is still paid to the MCN, and any withholding is taken from that payment"; the MCN receives a monthly report of total US taxes withheld; Google sends 1042-S/1099-MISC directly to affiliates based on the affiliate's own AdSense tax info — [YouTube Help, Tax requirements for MCNs and affiliate channels](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391273?hl=en)
- Brand Account roles: "An account must have one primary owner"; owners control who manages the account; managers can use Google services but cannot change the primary owner; primary ownership can be transferred only after being an owner for 7+ days; YouTube now recommends migrating to channel permissions instead of Brand Account roles — [YouTube Help, Manage Brand Account permissions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/4628007?hl=en)
- YPP entry thresholds for new applicants are reported to be changing from February 1, 2027 (an "8,000-hour" bar reported by one outlet; details not verified against Google) — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/new-youtube-creators-face-8-000-hour-bar-for-ad-revenue-from-february-2027/) (secondary; verify)
- Third-party MCN overview (2026): MCNs add services and scale but "don't replace YPP" — [Red Points](https://www.redpoints.com/?p=43417) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Three realistic operating models: (1) Partner-owned language channels — partner creates each channel (or the creator creates and transfers primary ownership to the partner after the 7-day owner wait), partner links its own AdSense, is the payee, and each channel must independently qualify for YPP (subscriber/watch-hour thresholds, with stricter reported thresholds from Feb. 2027 for new applicants); (2) Creator-owned MLA tracks — partner is a vendor; the creator's AdSense collects everything, and the partner invoices the creator (collection risk sits with the partner); (3) CMS/MCN — once the partner monetizes several channels, apply for Content Manager; language channels then become O&O content and, if the creator's main channel is linked as an affiliate, the partner receives all revenue and pays the creator per the affiliate agreement (YouTube requires written agreements and the ability to release affiliates — see MCN operations manual TOC).
- The MCN affiliate model also solves part of the tax plumbing (Google withholds US tax on affiliates' US-source earnings at the affiliate's own rate before paying the MCN), but creates a Chapter 3/1042-S obligation for the MCN on what it then pays out (see Q4).

### Gaps
- The "Set up your content owner as an MCN" and "Release channels" articles in the MCN operations manual were not retrieved; the exact application route (partner-manager invitation vs. form) is not confirmed by a primary source.
- No source found stating a minimum channel count/revenue for Content Manager approval in 2026.

## Key Question 4 — Revenue flow and splits, Analytics granularity by audio track, payment rails and tax (US withholding, Google Chapter 3 withholding, VAT, DAC7)

### Takeaway
YouTube does not report revenue by audio track: Analytics breaks out views and watch time by audio language, but the public YouTube Analytics API dimension list contains no audio-track/language dimension and revenue metrics cannot be split by it, so a partner relying on the creator's MLA tracks must contract for a formula-based share (e.g., watch-time-by-language proxy). Google withholds US tax on US-viewer revenue (up to 24% if no form; 30% Chapter 3 without treaty; 0–15% with treaty via W-8BEN); a US-based partner paying non-US creators is itself a withholding agent on US-source royalties (30% default, reduced by treaty with W-8BEN, reported on Form 1042-S); EU DAC7 and VAT treatment depend on whether the partner is a "platform operator" and on B2B place-of-supply rules.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube MLA analytics: "Use YouTube Analytics to track your views and watch time broken down by audio language to determine where demand exists"; no revenue-by-language reporting mentioned; MLA "currently available to creators with access to Advanced features"; tracks default to "the viewer's preferred language, which is determined by their watch history"; creators saw "over 25% of their watch time" from non-primary languages — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784?hl=en)
- YouTube Analytics API dimensions (official list): video, playlist, channel, group, country, province, dma, city, continent, subContinent, day, month, insightPlaybackLocationType/Detail, creatorContentType, liveOrOnDemand, subscribedStatus, youtubeProduct, insightTrafficSourceType/Detail, deviceType, operatingSystem, ageGroup, gender, sharingService, elapsedVideoTimeRatio, audienceType, livestreamPosition, membershipsCancellationSurveyReason, adType, claimedStatus, uploaderType — no audio-track, dub or caption-language dimension exists — [YouTube Analytics API, Dimensions](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/dimensions)
- Revenue metrics in the API (estimatedRevenue, estimatedAdRevenue, estimatedRedPartnerRevenue, grossRevenue, cpm, playbackBasedCpm) are governed by the `currency` parameter — [YouTube Analytics API, Reports: Query](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/v1/reports/query)
- Secondary coverage says creators "receive cumulative revenue from all language versions combined" under MLA — [netinfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-ai-dubbing-in-9-languages-for-creators/) (secondary; consistent with the absence of a per-track revenue dimension)
- Google/YouTube tax: all monetizing creators must submit tax info "regardless of their location in the world"; W-9 for US persons; W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E for non-US beneficial owners; W-8IMY for intermediaries/flow-throughs (all reviewed); "If tax info isn't provided, Google may be required to deduct up to 24% of your total earnings worldwide"; treaty income types listed as Other Copyright Royalties, Motion Picture & TV Royalties, Services; forms expire "at the end of the third full calendar year, after the year of signing"; entities claiming treaty benefits must certify the treaty's limitation-on-benefits clause; claim treaty benefits "by Dec. 10th" — [YouTube Help, Submitting your U.S. tax info to Google](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10390801?hl=en)
- Third-party summaries of Google's withholding tiers: up to 24% of worldwide earnings with no form; 30% of US-viewer earnings with a W-8BEN but no treaty; 0–15% with treaty (e.g., India 15%, UK and Germany 0% on royalties) — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-tax-info-w-8ben); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/non-u-s-creators-24-percent-tax-youtube-revenue/) (secondary)
- MCN context: affiliates' tax info drives withholding on the affiliate's revenue even though payment goes to the MCN; affiliates may seek refunds only for same-calendar-year withholding and refunds go "to the original content owner"; O&O content withholding uses the MCN's own AdSense tax info and appears in the AdSense Payments Report — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391273?hl=en)
- IRS: for US-source income not effectively connected with a US trade or business "the rate is usually 30%"; "Generally, you must withhold the tax at the time you pay the income to the foreign person"; Form 1042-S must be filed even where no tax is withheld because of a treaty; royalties are addressed in Publication 515 — [IRS, Withholding on specific income](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/withholding-on-specific-income)
- The beneficial owner claims a reduced treaty rate by giving Form W-8BEN to the withholding agent; an IRS practice unit addresses W-8BENs received after royalty payments were made — [IRS practice unit](https://irs.gov/pub/fatca/int_practice_units/RPW9424_01_03.pdf); [IRS, withholding on other US-source income](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/federal-income-tax-withholding-and-reporting-on-other-kinds-of-us-source-income-paid-to-nonresidents)
- DAC7 (Directive 2021/514): platform operators must collect, verify and report data on EU-resident "reportable sellers" conducting "relevant activities" (sale of goods, personal services, rental of property/transport); non-EU operators are in scope if they facilitate relevant activities of EU-resident sellers; reporting applies to cross-border and domestic activity — [Deloitte NL](https://www.deloitte.com/nl/en/services/tax/perspectives/dutch-dac7-implementation-increase-reporting-obligations-of-digital-platforms-on-platform-sellers.html); [Belastingdienst](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/business/content/information-for-platform-operators-dac7); [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2021/belgium/belgium-dac7-reporting-obligations-for-digital-platform-operators)
- Vendor split benchmarks: Videodubbing.com states it takes "30–50% of the revenue generated by your localized videos" as its fee (creator keeps 50–70%) — [videodubbing.com](https://videodubbing.com/revenue-share/); Subsub: "no upfront fees, we split revenue" (percentage not published) — [Subsub](https://www.subsub.io/content-export); Linguana: revenue share, percentage undisclosed — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/11/linguana-creators-localized-channels-ai-clone-voices/)
- Spotter's "Watch With" analogue: YouTube's Sept. 23, 2026 announcement lets reacting creators keep ad revenue from live reactions to others' long-form videos, with the original uploader getting attribution, view credit and control over which videos may be used — no revenue share to the original uploader and no equivalent mechanism described for dubs; live auto-dubbing pilot (English/Spanish) starts early 2027 — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-will-let-live-reactors-keep-ad-revenue-from-others-videos/)

### Inferences
- Model A (partner owns language channels; partner is AdSense payee) gives clean per-channel revenue data (Analytics/API by `channel` and `country`), and the creator's share can be computed from `estimatedRevenue` net of Google's withholding shown in the AdSense Payments Report. Model B (creator's MLA tracks) has no per-track revenue; the only defensible proxy is watch-time-by-audio-language from Studio (not available via the public API), so contracts should define the formula, require the creator to grant Studio analytics access (channel permissions "Viewer" role) and set audit rights.
- Tax stack for a US partner paying a non-US creator: (1) Google withholds on US-viewer revenue at the partner's own W-9/W-8 status when the partner is payee; (2) the partner's payment to the creator is a royalty for use of copyright and likeness — US-source to the extent the content is exploited in the US — so the partner must collect a W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E, withhold 30% or the treaty rate, file Forms 1042/1042-S, and should gross-up or pass through Google's withholding by contract. Double withholding risk (Google then partner) is real and should be addressed by characterizing the creator's share as the creator's income with the partner as a conduit, or by having the creator's channel be an affiliate so Google applies the creator's own W-8BEN. Treaty characterization (royalty vs. services) and foreign-tax-credit mechanics require advice.
- For a non-US (e.g., EU) partner: no US Chapter 3 obligation on payments to creators, but Google will withhold on the partner's US-viewer revenue at the partner's W-8BEN-E treaty rate; the partner should claim treaty benefits for "Other Copyright Royalties"/"Motion Picture & TV Royalties" and must re-certify every three years.
- DAC7: a bilateral licensing company that negotiates each deal and does not operate a marketplace where sellers connect with users is unlikely to be a "platform operator," but a self-serve marketplace matching creators and dubbers/voice actors could be. VAT: B2B licensing/dubbing services supplied cross-border within the EU are generally reverse-charged to the business customer; I found no source specific to creator-localization, so this is a flag, not a finding.
- Benchmarks suggest partner fees of 30–50% of localized-channel revenue (creator 50–70%) in vendor-published terms; a cost-recoup-first waterfall and minimum guarantees were not found in any published localization deal.

### Gaps
- No primary source for per-track revenue reporting in YouTube Studio (the help page only mentions views and watch time); per-language revenue appears unavailable.
- Business Insider/Bloomberg reporting with hard split percentages for Jellysmack, Spotter or Linguana was not retrieved; the 80/20 Jellysmack figure is unverified.
- Current IRS Publication 515 text and specific treaty royalty rates were not fetched; the 0%/15% figures come from secondary creator-tax guides.
- No authoritative source on VAT treatment (reverse charge/place of supply) or DAC7 applicability specific to dubbing partnerships.

## Key Question 5 — Contract-term benchmarks (splits, term, exclusivity, minimum guarantees, approvals, termination, indemnities) and reused-content/strike risk mitigation, including whether YouTube offers any "authorized dub" linkage

### Takeaway
Published benchmarks are thin: vendor terms cluster at 30–50% partner fee on localized-channel revenue with no upfront cost, catalog financiers used ~5-year terms, and no public source documents minimum guarantees or exclusivity-by-language clauses; YouTube offers no "authorized dub" cross-channel linkage, its reused-content (now "inauthentic content") policy applies "even if you have permission," and the creator-authorized mechanisms that do exist (MLA tracks on the creator's own channel; Content Manager/MCN claims) are the only platform-recognized ways to prove authorized reuse — so the contract and documentation have to carry the burden.

### Cited Findings
- YPP policy renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025; "no change to our reused content policy"; reused content = repurposing content already online "without significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value"; the policy "applies even if you have permission from the original creator"; not allowed: "Content uploaded many times by other creators"; allowed: "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary"; reviewers look at videos, channel description, titles and descriptions and the policy may apply if they "cannot clearly tell that you made the content"; enforcement is channel-level ("monetization may be removed from your entire channel") — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Copyright strikes are distinct: a strike follows a rights holder's takedown; a retraction or a valid counter notification can clear it; "Misuse of our processes, such as submitting fraudulent documentation, may result in the termination of your account" — [YouTube Help, Submit a copyright counter notification](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2807684?hl=en)
- Practitioner guidance on documentation to keep for disputes: "license agreements, cue sheets, email permissions, invoices, chain of title summaries" — [Daily Rind](https://dailyrindblog.com/?p=56551) (secondary)
- Reused-content rejection guidance (2026): channels with zero Content ID claims can still fail YPP review; permission does not cure lack of original contribution — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected) (secondary)
- Content Manager permissions let a content owner claim videos on linked channels and apply monetization policies — the platform-native way an authorized party "owns" dubbed uploads — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6351520?hl=en)
- "Watch With" (Sept. 2026) gives original uploaders "control over which videos can be used" by reactors, with attribution and view credit but no revenue share; nothing equivalent is described for dubs — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-will-let-live-reactors-keep-ad-revenue-from-others-videos/)
- Term benchmarks: Jellysmack catalog licenses "up to five years" — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/01/25/jellysmack-500-million-licensing-old-youtube-videos/); Spotter "license their content to Spotter for five years" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/spotter-invest-youtubers-back-catalog/)
- Split benchmarks: 30–50% partner fee (Videodubbing.com) — [videodubbing.com](https://videodubbing.com/revenue-share/); undisclosed revenue share, no upfront fee, only with creators with "strong momentum" (Linguana) — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/11/linguana-creators-localized-channels-ai-clone-voices/); human dubbing cost benchmark $20–$50/min — same Tubefilter article
- AB 2602 requires replica-use clauses to describe intended uses "with reasonable specificity" (relevant to approval/scope drafting) — [Covington](https://www.covingtonblogs.com/2024/09/11/california-passes-digital-replica-legislation-as-congress-considers-federal-approach/)

### Inferences
- Term sheet checklist derived from the above: (a) Grant: non-exclusive on the original; exclusive per listed language (and optionally per territory, though YouTube serves MLA by viewer language, not geography) for a 3–5 year term with auto-renewal; (b) Economics: partner fee 30–50% of net channel revenue (after Google's cut and Google's US withholding), optional recoupable advance; minimum guarantee only if the partner is confident in catalog data (Spotter model prices MG as a multiple of trailing revenue); (c) Approvals: creator approval of voice-clone samples and the first N dubs per language, then deemed approval after X days; (d) Branding: license to use channel name/logo/thumbnails with mandatory "official [language] channel" labelling and cross-links; (e) Termination: automatic if the creator's main channel is terminated or the creator loses YPP; cure periods for strikes; (f) Post-term: wind-down of dubbed channels or assignment of channels to the creator, with voice-model deletion; (g) Reps/warranties: creator owns or controls all content, music is cleared for new channels or will be replaced, no conflicting catalog license (Spotter/Jellysmack-type), compliance with AB 2602-style formalities; (h) Indemnity: creator indemnifies for third-party IP claims in the original; partner indemnifies for dub-specific additions and for AI/disclosure compliance; (i) Content ID: creator agrees not to claim dubbed uploads and to whitelist partner channels in any CMS; partner agrees to respond to erroneous strikes; (j) Data: creator grants Studio analytics access; audit rights; monthly statements.
- Reused-content mitigation: the dubbed channel must be visibly "made by" the licensee — the channel description, titles, pinned video and About page should state the licensing relationship and link both ways; the dubbed version should add substantive modification (new voice track, localized on-screen text, localized intro/outro). Keep the signed license, voice consent, music licenses/safelists and chain-of-title summary ready to submit to Creator Support if YPP review flags reused content. Note YouTube's policy text expressly does not treat permission as a cure.
- Where the partner is an MCN with CMS, linking the creator's main channel as an affiliate and registering the dubs as partner-claimed assets is the closest thing to an "authorized dub" linkage YouTube offers; otherwise MLA tracks on the creator's channel are the only officially linked dubs.

### Gaps
- No public source documents minimum guarantees, exclusivity-by-language, approval, or indemnity clauses in actual dubbing deals; the checklist above is inference from adjacent deals and policy text.
- No documented cases found of how specific partners (Linguana, Subsub, Spotter-funded teams) handled strikes or reused-content rejections on dubbed channels.
- YouTube offers no feature to link an authorized third-party dubbed channel to the original (none found in Help Center or 2026 announcements).
