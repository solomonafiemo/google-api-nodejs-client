# The "Localization Partner" Business Model for YouTube (state as of November 2026)

Scope note: Covers companies that take an established creator's catalogue, dub/localize it, run separate language channels (or supply multi-audio tracks) and share revenue. All figures dated; vendor claims and estimates are labelled. Several primary sources (Slator interviews, Variety) returned HTTP 403 on fetch; where only search-snippet text was available this is flagged. Research conducted 2026-11-14.

## Key Question 1: Jellysmack — model, funding/valuation, layoffs/pivots 2022-2025, status in 2026, lessons

### Takeaway
Jellysmack never became a dubbing/localization company in any documented way; its two creator-facing products (the cross-platform Creator Program redistributing creators' libraries to Facebook/Snap/TikTok/YouTube for a rev share, and the $500M JellyFi catalog-licensing fund) were respectively scaled back (2024) and sold (May 2024). By 2026 it is an owned-media holding company (Law&Crime, Court TV, Blue Foxes spin-out) with ~222 staff, and its reported valuation has fallen below the $1B it carried in 2021. The documented lesson from former staff: top creators would not "rock the boat" on their YouTube revenue, and Spotter out-bid Jellysmack on catalog deals.

### Cited Findings
- Jellysmack was founded as Keli Network in 2016 and became known for helping top YouTubers including MrBeast and PewDiePie distribute content across platforms; it received a $500M investment from SoftBank in 2021 — [Net Influencer, 17 May 2024](https://www.netinfluencer.com/jellysmack-failed-jellyfi-500m-creator-buyout-plan/)
- Fortune (Oct 2022) covered Jellysmack and Spotter together as venture-backed "unicorn" bets on the creator economy — [Fortune, 21 Oct 2022](https://fortune.com/2022/10/21/jellysmack-spotter-unicorn-startups-venture-capital-bet-creator-economy) (headline/URL only; body not fetched)
- Creator Program model: Jellysmack redistributed creators' video libraries across Facebook, Snapchat, TikTok and YouTube in exchange for a share of ad revenue; JellyFi (launched early 2022) made upfront payments to license future ad revenue from creators' existing YouTube libraries, with $500M set aside — [Publisher in a Box, 2026](https://publisherinabox.com/alternatives-to-jellysmack)
- The Creator Program revenue split was never disclosed and was negotiated per deal — [FindClout review, 2026](https://findclout.com/blog/jellysmack) (low-authority affiliate blog)
- JellyFi was sold to Copyright Capital; both companies confirmed to Business Insider; terms undisclosed; article dated 17 May 2024 — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/jellysmack-failed-jellyfi-500m-creator-buyout-plan/)
- Former employee quote: "creators do not want to do anything to rock the boat on their YouTube revenue"; two other former employees said Jellysmack's offers were often less lucrative than Spotter's; a Jellysmack spokesperson said JellyFi "generated revenue but was deprioritized as other initiatives proved more profitable" — [Net Influencer, 17 May 2024](https://www.netinfluencer.com/jellysmack-failed-jellyfi-500m-creator-buyout-plan/)
- Oct 2024: Jellysmack confirmed ~22 US employees laid off with further French cuts expected; scaled back its Creator Program on Meta, prioritizing business units managing owned IP (YouTube channels Gamology, House of Bounce, Beauty Studio, Oh My Goal); one report attributed the Meta pullback to "opacity" in Meta's new monetization model — [SPEEDA Edge, Oct 2024](https://sp-edge.com/updates/35125); [TechStory](https://techstory.in/softbank-backed-startup-jellysmack-layoffs-staff-due-to-restructuring/)
- A December 2022 layoff round is reported after "a contraction in monetization across several social media platforms" — [Everything-PR, 2026](https://everything-pr.com/jellysmack-the-creator-distribution-engine-operating-at-scale) (secondary; the same site says "material layoffs in 2023")
- Glassdoor former-employee reviews cite "frequent layoffs" in 21 reviews — [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Reviews/Employee-Review-Jellysmack-E2424088-RVW69082918.htm) (anecdotal)
- June 2023: Jellysmack and Culture Genesis announced a $25M initiative for underrepresented YouTube creators — [BusinessWire, 20 Jun 2023](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20230620197403/en/Culture-Genesis-and-Jellysmack-Launch-25M-Initiative-to-Boost-Underrepresented-YouTube-Creators)
- 2025: Jellysmack spun a large part of its owned-content business (Gamology, House of Bounce, Oh My Goal) into "Blue Foxes" — [Publisher in a Box, 2026](https://publisherinabox.com/alternatives-to-jellysmack) (single source)
- Feb 2026: E.W. Scripps agreed to sell Court TV to Law&Crime, "now owned by media creator holding company Jellysmack"; the NYT reported the pact values Court TV at under $125M; terms not officially disclosed — [Variety, Feb 2026](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/scripps-sells-court-tv-lawandcrime-dan-abrams-jellysmack-1236657158/); [The Desk, Feb 2026](https://thedesk.net/2026/02/scripps-sells-court-tv-to-lawcrime-owner-jellysmack/); [TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/business/scripps-selling-court-tv-to-jellysmack)
- Tracxn lists Jellysmack headcount at 222 as of 31 May 2026 and 2021 as the latest funding round — [Tracxn, 2026](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/jellysmack/__PHmo3F7nSp6x9do4fI665DjS3K1MoRNb6rM6RFbPgyY)
- An aggregator reports a $35M Jellysmack funding round in March 2026 — [SignalBase](https://www.trysignalbase.com/news/funding/jellysmack-raises-35) (unconfirmed; no detail; conflicts with Tracxn)
- The 2026 "alternatives" article concludes Jellysmack has "largely walked away from" both the Creator Program and catalog licensing — [Publisher in a Box, 2026](https://publisherinabox.com/alternatives-to-jellysmack)

### Inferences
- Jellysmack's trajectory is evidence that operating other people's catalogues for a rev share was not durable at venture scale: platform monetization changes (Meta) collapsed the distribution arm, and the capital-intensive catalog arm lost on price to Spotter. The company survived by converting to owning IP outright.
- Nothing found indicates Jellysmack ever ran dubbed-language channels at scale; it is not a precedent for the localization-partner model specifically, but it is the closest precedent for "operate a creator's catalogue elsewhere for a rev share."

### Gaps
- No primary source for the 2021 valuation figure (widely reported as ~$1B; one unvetted GitHub compilation cites ~$3B peak per PitchBook; CB Insights lists only $52M raised) — treat all valuation figures as unverified.
- The June 2026 "Mighty Nine" study reporting Jellysmack fell below €1B appeared only in search-result text with no retrievable URL.
- No published account of Creator Program revenue split percentages or of Jellysmack localized/dubbed channels.

## Key Question 2: Spotter — catalog licensing, 2026 fixed-% AdSense offering, scale, localization

### Takeaway
Spotter remains the dominant catalog-licensing player (company claims $1B in creator payouts across 800+ channels as of 2026), and its current product is explicitly an upfront lump sum in exchange for a fixed percentage of long-form AdSense until Spotter hits a target return. It does not advertise localization or dubbing; its 2025-2026 expansion is into ads ("Creator TV" upfronts) and studio/streaming programming.

### Cited Findings
- Feb 2022: Spotter raised $200M Series D led by SoftBank at a $1.7B valuation, with a stated plan to invest $1B in creators' back catalogs by mid-2023 — [TechCrunch, 16 Feb 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/spotter-invest-youtubers-back-catalog/)
- Sept 2022: Tubefilter reported Spotter had deployed $600M — [Tubefilter, 19 Sep 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/09/19/spotter-catalog-licensing-youtube-fund-600-million-billion/)
- Spotter Capital page (2026): "for the duration of the deal, Spotter shares the long-form YouTube AdSense revenue at a fixed percentage"; "once Spotter hits its return, 100% of the ad revenue goes back to you"; "$1 Billion" in "creator payouts through successful licensing deals"; "800+" channels funded; "over 80% of creators explore a continued partnership after their first deal"; long-form required (Shorts-only ineligible), creators must own all rights; no mention of localization or dubbing — [Spotter Creator Capital](https://www.spotter.com/creator-capital) (company claims)
- Reported Spotter deal sizes range from ~$15,000 to $40M, averaging ~$1.5M, with typical ~5-year terms; the same article says ~$350M spent since 2019 with a $1B target (likely stale vs. Spotter's own $1B claim) — [Publisher in a Box, 2026](https://publisherinabox.com/alternatives-to-jellysmack)
- DeBevoise has said Spotter's target payback is around four years — [Fast Company, 2022](https://www.fastcompany.com/90731575/why-this-company-is-investing-1-billion-in-your-favorite-youtube-creators-like-mr-beast)
- Spotter Studio (AI subscription product) moved in-house in Oct 2025; Spotter expanded into scripted streaming via Kinigra Deon's project on Tubi in Dec 2025 — [OutlierKit MCN list, 2026](https://outlierkit.com/resources/best-youtube-mcns/) (secondary)
- Spotter Showcase, 4 March 2026, New York: "Creator TV" pitch to brands, with Dude Perfect, Kinigra Deon, Jordan Matter, Airrack, Michelle Khare; business lines mentioned are Spotter Ads and creator programming only — [Tubefilter, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/02/18/spotter-showcase-2026-new-york-creator-tv-upfront/); [Tubefilter, 6 Mar 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/03/06/spotter-showcase-creator-tv-brand-content-integration/)
- Spotter data presentation: 6,600 YouTube creators now rival prime-time TV in reach — [PPC Land, 2026](https://ppc.land/spotters-data-reveals-6-600-youtube-creators-now-rival-prime-time-tv-in-reach/)

### Inferences
- Spotter's "fixed % of long-form AdSense" is a financing product (effectively a revenue-based loan secured on AdSense), not an operating/localization service. A localization partner is complementary, not competitive, to Spotter — and Spotter's payee position on a creator's main-channel AdSense could complicate a partner's claim on dubbed-track revenue on that same channel (multi-audio tracks pay into the main channel's AdSense).

### Gaps
- The actual fixed percentage Spotter takes is not public.
- No independent audit of the "$1B deployed/paid out" figure.
- No evidence Spotter funds or operates dubbed channels.

## Key Question 3: Dubbed-channel case studies with numbers (MrBeast, Dhar Mann, Kurzgesagt, Veritasium, Dude Perfect, Unilingo results) and RPM by language

### Takeaway
Separate dubbed channels for A-list creators reach the mid-single-digit to mid-20s millions of subscribers (MrBeast en Español ~26.8M, Apr 2026; MrBeast Brasil ~6.6M; Kurzgesagt German 2.69M; Veritasium Spanish 2.96M), i.e. typically 5-15% of the main channel. Unilingo's own cumulative figures (10.1B views, ~$11M added ad revenue, 55M subscribers across clients) imply ~$1.1 RPM on dubbed views — consistent with the fact that most dubbed views land in low-CPM markets (LatAm/Brazil/India at roughly $0.5-$1 per 1,000 views vs. ~$7 US, per AIR Media-Tech's 2025 data).

### Cited Findings — MrBeast
- Unilingo signed MrBeast in 2021; MrBeast en Español had 23M subscribers in March 2023; Unilingo hired the Spanish voice of Spider-Man and, for Japanese, Junko Takeuchi (voice of Naruto), which took six months to arrange — [Rest of World, 6 Mar 2023](https://restofworld.org/2023/youtube-foreign-language-dub/)
- MrBeast en Español: ~26.8M subscribers and 67 videos as of early April 2026; channel created 9 July 2021 — [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/c/mrbeastenespanol) (third-party tracker)
- MrBeast Brasil: ~6.6M subscribers, ~865M views, 77 videos; created 27 Sep 2021 — [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/mrbeastbrasil/achievements) (undated snapshot)
- March 2025: MrBeast said ~70% of his YouTube audience does not speak English; he now uses AI to dub his own voice rather than voice actors and says retention is ~1% higher because viewers hear his voice; he earns "tens of millions" of views from Spanish and Portuguese speakers; he complained Meta lacks multi-audio tracks — [Fortune, 28 Mar 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/03/28/mrbeast-advice-mark-zuckerberg-meta-video-dubbing)
- Night CEO Reed Duchscher (MrBeast's manager): 15% of MrBeast's watch time comes from non-primary-language users; recent quote "Creators are now starting to realize how expensive dubbing videos are." — [Human Voice Over blog citing Duchscher LinkedIn](https://www.humanvoiceover.com/blog/night-media-exposes-the-truth-about-multi-language-audio-on-youtube) (vendor blog relaying a LinkedIn post; undated)
- MrBeast's Spanish content was part of YouTube's original multi-language audio tests (2021) — [Rest of World, 2023](https://restofworld.org/2023/youtube-foreign-language-dub/)
- A 2023 Slator piece reported MrBeast's early trial dubbed his most popular videos into 12+ languages — [Slator](https://slator.com/why-mrbeast-is-launching-a-dubbing-company/) (fetch returned 403; from search snippet)

### Cited Findings — Other creators
- Veritasium: hired Unilingo in 2018 for Spanish; the Spanish channel had earned over $50,000 in extra revenue and 200M+ views by March 2023; Unilingo handled seven languages — [Rest of World, 2023](https://restofworld.org/2023/youtube-foreign-language-dub/)
- Veritasium en español: 2.96M subscribers as of 23 Oct 2026 (+10K in the prior month) vs. main channel 21.4M (Oct 2026) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@veritasiumes/); [Wikipedia: Derek Muller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Muller)

- Dude Perfect en Español: ~221K subscribers, ~41M views (undated tracker) vs. main channel ~62.3M (Aug 2026); the Spanish channel uses voice actors — [Thoughtleaders](https://app.thoughtleaders.io/youtube/dude-perfect-en-espanol); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/dudeperfect); Dude Perfect is a Unilingo client per [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/youtube-foreign-language-dub/)
- Dhar Mann Studios: content translated into 13 languages; estimated $65M revenue in 2025 from YouTube AdSense, Facebook in-stream ads and brand deals — [Forbes, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/stevenbertoni/2026/06/23/how-top-creator-dhar-mann-built-the-hollywood-studio-of-the-future/); an earlier CEO interview said seven languages with audiences in India and the Philippines — [Vitrina](https://vitrina.ai/blog/dhar-mann-studios-top-digital-businesses/); fan wikis list separate Portuguese, Spanish, Hindi, Arabic, Filipino→Turkish, and French channels — [Dhar Mann fandom wiki](https://dubdb.fandom.com/wiki/Dhar_Mann) (unverified)

### Inferences
- Unilingo's own aggregate numbers (10.1B views → $11M added revenue) imply roughly $1.09 of ad revenue per 1,000 dubbed views, which matches the AIR country table for LatAm/Brazil/India. Dubbed channels are a volume business in low-CPM markets; only German, French, Japanese and Korean dubs approach Anglo-market monetization.
- MrBeast en Español having only 67 uploads in ~5 years (vs. a far larger main-channel catalogue) suggests the separate channel is selective and that most of his localization now flows through multi-audio tracks on the main channel (he was in the original MLA test and said in 2025 he uses AI voice clones). This is an inference, not a documented decision.

### Gaps
- No public figure for what MrBeast paid for human dubbing (per video or per minute); his only cost-related statements are indirect (Night: "expensive").
- No Mark Rober or Zach King dubbed-channel data found.
- Dhar Mann: no breakdown of international/dubbed-channel revenue within the $65M estimate.
- No official YouTube RPM-by-language data exists; all figures above are vendor-sourced.
- Kurzgesagt Spanish channel figures not retrieved.

## Key Question 4: YouTube's multi-language audio and auto-dubbing 2025-2026 — availability, quality, effect on separate-language channels, revenue attribution

### Takeaway
As of Feb 4, 2026 auto-dubbing is on by default for all creators in 27 languages, with "Expressive Speech" in 8 languages and lip-sync in a limited pilot; YouTube reports 6M+ daily viewers watching 10+ minutes of auto-dubbed content (Dec 2025) and that dubbed views average ~75% of the original's view duration. YouTube's documentation says nothing about how dubbed-track revenue is attributed and does not state a preference for tracks over separate channels; practitioners say dubbed-track revenue accrues at the viewer's country ad rate.

### Cited Findings — Rollout timeline
- Aloud (Google Area 120 dubbing tool) launched 9 Mar 2022; auto dubbing formally launched 10 Dec 2024 for knowledge-focused YPP channels in eight languages; Sept 18, 2024 expansion to "hundreds of thousands" of creators; April 2025 all YPP creators could self-enrol; 10 Sep 2025 multi-language audio opened to millions of creators after a two-year pilot; 4 Feb 2026 general availability in 27 languages; 15 May 2026 Creator Insider performance details; 23 Sep 2026 live auto-dubbing announced for early 2027 — [PPC Land explainer](https://ppc.land/auto-dubbing/) (secondary timeline; the Sept 2026 live-dubbing item is single-source)
- YouTube blog, 4 Feb 2026 (Chandralekha Motati): auto dubbing "is now available to everyone"; 27 languages; Expressive Speech on all channels in English, French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish; Lip Sync pilot "subtly matches the speaker's lip movements"; in December [2025] YouTube averaged 6M+ daily viewers watching at least 10 minutes of auto-dubbed content; smart filtering skips music/silent videos; creators can supply their own dubs or turn it off; no statement about revenue or about separate channels — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-auto-dubbing-expressive-speech/)
- Dubbed versions average about 75% of the original's view duration; videos over 120 minutes are ineligible — [PPC Land, 18 May 2026](https://ppc.land/youtube-auto-dubbing-tops-6m-daily-viewers-expands-to-27-languages/)
- YouTube Help: automatic dubbing is enabled by default for eligible creators; ineligible if >120 min, little speech, unsupported/undetectable language, speech too fast, or if it "contains copyrighted content"; dubs cannot be edited (fix the source language and regenerate); expressive speech "isn't always applied"; lip sync is "experimental" for select channels via "Allow experimental features"; the page does not cover monetization — [YouTube Help: Automatic dubbing](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15569972)
- Neal Mohan's Feb 11, 2025 annual letter: for videos with dubbed audio, over 40% of total watch time comes from viewers choosing to listen in a different language — [Google blog (Mohan letter)](https://blog.google/intl/en-mena/company-news/inside-google/from-the-youtube-ceo-our-big-bets-for-2025/); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/youtube-tv-viewing-overtakes-mobile-ceo-neal-mohan-1236132074/)
- Multi-language audio two-year pilot (human-recorded tracks): over 25% of watch time from non-primary languages; tripled views for Jamie Oliver's channel; MLA "does not automatically create these tracks" — it is a delivery container for human/vendor tracks — [PPC Land explainer](https://ppc.land/auto-dubbing/)
- Lip Sync pilot reportedly limited to 5 languages and 1080p — [Dubly.ai](https://dubly.ai/blog/youtube-announces-auto-dubbing-with-lip-sync---but-is-it-really-worth-it) (competitor vendor; unverified)

### Cited Findings — Quality evidence (vendor data)
- AIR Media-Tech retention comparison (updated 28 Sep 2026): on the same videos, AI-only tracks held viewers "4 to 10 times less" than pro-dubbed tracks in several cases — Brave Wilderness pro-dub AVD 3:40-5:19 vs 1:22 for the English auto-dub; an Italian kids' channel 5-6 min vs 0:54; a Serbian channel 5:51 (German pro) vs 0:43 (English AI). Counter-example: an 80M-view Spanish channel's AI-dubbed English/French/German/Italian tracks held 4:01-4:27 vs 5:34 for the original (May 2025-Apr 2026); DenLion's AI Arabic track (3:24) outperformed the Spanish original (3:19) — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/is-youtubes-ai-dubbing-safe-for-your-channel-auto-dub-vs-pro-with-real-retention-data) (vendor selling pro dubbing)
- Linguana CMO Jonny Steel: YouTube's default AI dub tracks are "pretty flat and missing out on your emotion and personality" and sometimes muddled with multiple speakers — [Tubefilter, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/11/linguana-creators-localized-channels-ai-clone-voices/) (Linguana is a Tubefilter partner)

### Cited Findings — Revenue attribution and tracks vs. channels
- PPC Land: "YouTube's automatic dubbing documentation does not address how advertising or revenue works on dubbed tracks" — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/auto-dubbing/); TimedSubs likewise found no stated monetization bonus or penalty — [TimedSubs](https://timedsubs.com/en/guides/youtube-auto-dubbing)
- Practitioner guidance: a dubbed view is monetized at the ad rate of the viewer's market, so lower-CPM dubbed views raise total revenue while pulling blended RPM down; revenue from translated tracks "accumulates the same way as revenue from the original videos" — [MilX](https://milx.app/en/cases/can-adding-new-language-reset-a-youtube-monetization-algorithm); [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/youtube-multilingual-dubbing-auto-manual/)
- Rest of World (2023): before MLA, creators uploaded separate channels per language; "some may keep doing this to work around YouTube's recommendation algorithm" — [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/youtube-foreign-language-dub/)
- AIR Media-Tech's Dennis Batyrev (2023): test languages as dub tracks on the main channel first, then spin a popular language off into its own channel — [Tubefilter, 1 Aug 2023](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/08/01/youtube-content-language-localization-air-media-tech/)
- Linguana (vendor, 11 Jul 2025): separate channels recommended for creators at ~1M+ monthly views; claims localized channels may qualify for ad inventory "often with higher CPMs"; cites Mohan's 40% figure — [Linguana insights](https://www.linguana.com/insights/the-smart-way-to-localize-on-youtube)
- A Q&A noted Colin & Samir could not use a separate-channel approach because "the video needs to be the same length" (for tracks) — [The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/mateo-price-qanda) (indirect, weak)

### Inferences
- YouTube has given no public preference for tracks over channels and has not restricted separate dubbed channels; but by making dubbing free, default-on and viewer-language-targeted, it has removed the "no dub exists" gap that separate channels filled, leaving them to compete on quality, localized packaging (title/thumbnail) and algorithmic separation.
- The 75% AVD ratio for auto-dubs vs. AIR's 4-10x retention gap are not contradictory: the 75% figure is a platform-wide average, while AIR's examples are high-energy/kids content where voice performance matters most.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement found on (a) whether revenue from a dubbed track is paid at the viewer's country CPM (practitioner consensus says yes), or (b) whether YouTube prefers tracks to separate channels.
- No documented cases found of creators consolidating existing dubbed channels into main-channel tracks after Feb 2026.
- No independent (non-vendor) retention comparison of auto-dub vs. pro dub.

## Key Question 5: Policy risk — reused content, duplicates, Content ID between original and dubbed channels

### Takeaway
YouTube's reused-content policy does not mention dubs or translations, applies "even if you have permission from the original creator," and is judged channel-wide; third-party guides report occasional reused-content flags on creators' own dubbed second channels. Operators like Linguana (1,200+ creators) and AIR (400+ localized channels) run separate dubbed channels at scale without any reported mass enforcement, but that is absence of reporting, not an official safe harbour.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube monetization policy text: "Reused content refers to channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source" without significant original commentary; "This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator"; "Our reused content policy applies to your channel as a whole"; "If we cannot tell that the content is yours, it may be subject to our reused content policy"; the "repetitious content" policy was renamed "inauthentic content" on 15 Jul 2025; translations/dubs are not mentioned — [YouTube Help: Channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Third-party guides: creators uploading dubbed versions of their own videos to a second channel "sometimes get a reused content flag"; the policy "doesn't carve out your own earlier uploads"; reused-content review and Content ID are separate checks — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected); [NoobClaw](https://noobclaw.com/blog/same-video-two-youtube-channels-2026/); [Creator Essentials](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/reused-content/) (secondary)
- Content ID: if the original owner uses Content ID, matches on other channels are claimed unless the owner allowlists that channel — [Creator Essentials](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/reused-content/); Mansorian's early PewDiePie fandub channel (100K subs) was taken down by copyright request before he partnered officially — [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/youtube-foreign-language-dub/)
- AIR reports YouTube terminated 16 channels with 35M combined subscribers in January 2026, "all without a human behind the content" (faceless/AI channels, not dubs) — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/is-youtubes-ai-dubbing-safe-for-your-channel-auto-dub-vs-pro-with-real-retention-data) (single source)
- Auto dubbing is ineligible for videos that contain copyrighted content — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15569972)
- Linguana is listed in YouTube's official Services Directory — [YouTube Services Directory](https://servicesdirectory.withyoutube.com/directory/linguana) (suggests the separate-channel model is at least tolerated)

### Inferences
- A licensed partner mitigates reused-content risk by making the dubbed channel visibly the creator's official channel (branding, links, verified ownership), publishing the creator's own voice clone, and ensuring the creator's Content ID assets allowlist the dubbed channel. Because the policy applies channel-wide, one flagged channel does not endanger the main channel, but the partner's entire network per creator is exposed.

### Gaps
- No YouTube statement on dubbed re-uploads by licensed partners specifically.
- No documented enforcement action against a major dubbed-channel network in 2025-2026 was found.

## Key Question 6: Economics of a localization partner — revenue splits, cost per minute, fixed costs, breakeven, and startups with 2025-2026 traction

### Takeaway
Current rev-share partners take an undisclosed cut (vendors quote 30-50% of dubbed-channel revenue; Unilingo variously described at 10% cut or 30-50% to the creator), with zero upfront cost to the creator, voice-clone AI dubbing, and a channel-per-language operating model; Linguana (seed $8.5M, Apr 2025; 1,200+ creators, 41 languages by Jun 2026) is the standout 2025-2026 example, reporting "tens of thousands of USD/month" per top creator. Human dubbing is quoted at $20-$50/min (Linguana) up to $50-$300+/min (vendors), Netflix's rate card ~$6/min; AI dubbing is near-zero marginal cost but needs QA and metadata localization.

### Cited Findings — Revenue splits
- Unilingo (2023): upfront fee + 10% of future dubbed-channel ad revenue — [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/youtube-foreign-language-dub/); Unilingo founder: creators get "around 30-50%" — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/farbodmansorian_unilingo-was-the-first-company-to-provide-activity-7062875332013420544-Tu4Q); Unilingo pricing: rev share plus monthly maintenance fee — [Unilingo](https://www.unilingo.co/pricing)
- Linguana: no upfront cost; "keeps a cut of AdSense revenue"; percentage not disclosed — [Tubefilter, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/06/24/linguana-content-localization-distribution-monetization-in-2026/)
- videodubbing.com: receives 30-50% of revenue generated by its localized videos — [videodubbing.com](https://videodubbing.com/revenue-share/) (vendor)
- DittoDub (software, no rev share) frames rev-share deals as "typically 50%" and models partner shares of 25-30% in illustrative examples (e.g., $20,000 earnings → $6,000 partner share vs $3,500 owned-workflow cost) — [DittoDub Medium](https://medium.com/@dittodub/dont-give-up-50-of-your-revenue-why-rev-share-ai-dubbing-is-a-bad-deal-for-creators-bf5f3536c491); [DittoDub article](https://dittodub.com/articles/rev-share-ai-dubbing-bad-deal-for-creators) (competitor advocacy)

### Cited Findings — Cost per minute
- Professional voice acting "sometimes $20 to $50 per minute" — Linguana via [Tubefilter, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/11/linguana-creators-localized-channels-ai-clone-voices/)

- Vendor ranges for human dubbing: $50 to $300+/min — [DittoDub](https://dittodub.com/articles/26_07_2025-ai-dubbing-mrbeast-s-secret-to-400m-subscribers); $75-$300 per finished minute per language — [Spimov, 2025](https://spimov.com/blog/en/video-localization-cost-2025) (both vendors selling AI)
- ElevenLabs Dubbing v2: launched 28 May 2026, 90+ languages, carries original emotion/performance; "Creator Dubbing Partner Program" gives eligible creators discounted access (discount and criteria unspecified); no per-minute price published on the post; ElevenProductions pairs it with human translators/casting — [ElevenLabs blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/introducing-dubbing-v2)
- ElevenStudios (Aug 2024): fully managed dubbing with bilingual experts for Colin & Samir, Drew Binsky, Jon Youshaei, Ali Abdaal in Spanish, Portuguese, Arabic, French (some coverage adds Hindi) — [ElevenLabs blog](https://beta.elevenlabs.io/blog/elevenstudios-creator-launch)

- ElevenLabs–Meta partnership (Dec 2025) for dubbing Reels in local languages — [ElevenLabs on X](https://x.com/ElevenLabs/status/1999163506743038408)

### Inferences
- Illustrative breakeven (my estimate using AIR's country figures): a Spanish/Portuguese dubbed channel earning ~$0.8-$1.1 per 1,000 views grosses ~$800-$1,100 per million views; at a 30-50% partner share the partner nets ~$250-$550 per million views. A partner therefore needs tens of millions of monthly dubbed views per language cluster (or German/French/Japanese tilt at $3-$5 per 1,000) to cover QA, metadata, community and channel ops staff — consistent with Linguana only taking "already established" creators and quoting ~1M+ monthly views as the threshold.
- Bobby's 100M views in ~4 months across 14 channels (~25M/month) generating "tens of thousands USD/month" implies a blended ~$1-$2 RPM at the channel level (before split), again consistent with LatAm-heavy traffic.
- The viable 2026 operating model has converged: AI voice clone (not actors) + human QA + per-language channel + localized metadata + off-YouTube syndication (MSN, Spotify, Dailymotion) + multilingual brand-deal sales. Pure dubbing-as-a-service (Papercup's original model) has been absorbed by larger vendors or commoditized by YouTube/ElevenLabs.

## Key Question 7: Competitive erosion — what value remains for a partner as auto-dubbing improves; evidence of creators switching either way

### Takeaway
No reporting was found of creators dropping a localization partner for YouTube auto-dub, nor of the reverse; the observable 2025-2026 market response is that partners repositioned around what auto-dub does not do: cloned-voice performance quality, localized titles/thumbnails/descriptions, separate algorithmic surfaces per language, off-YouTube distribution and multilingual ad sales — while MrBeast himself moved from hired voice actors to AI clones of his own voice, and his manager says creators are realizing "how expensive dubbing videos are."

### Cited Findings
- MrBeast (Mar 2025) switched from voice actors to AI dubbing of his own voice, citing ~1% higher retention — [Fortune, 28 Mar 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/03/28/mrbeast-advice-mark-zuckerberg-meta-video-dubbing)
- Night CEO Reed Duchscher: "Creators are now starting to realize how expensive dubbing videos are." — [HVO citing Duchscher](https://www.humanvoiceover.com/blog/night-media-exposes-the-truth-about-multi-language-audio-on-youtube) (vendor relay; undated)
- Night nonetheless referred its client Bobby to Linguana's separate-channel model in Sep 2025 — [Tubefilter, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/14/youtube-content-localization-data-bassfishingproductions-linguana/)
- Auto-dubbing does not translate titles/descriptions (AIR advises translating them manually); DittoDub's Chrome extension automates localized titles, descriptions, subtitles and thumbnails — [AIR](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/is-youtubes-ai-dubbing-safe-for-your-channel-auto-dub-vs-pro-with-real-retention-data); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/inside-dittodub-the-ai-dubbing-startup-behind-erling-haaland-multilingual-youtube-push/)
- Linguana's stated differentiators vs. default AI dubs: emotional replication in a cloned voice, speaker/age distinction, "dozens of steps" of processing, hands-off channel operations, MSN/Spotify/Dailymotion syndication, AI-generated multilingual ad inserts; "The biggest asset we have is our relationships with creators" — [Tubefilter, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/11/linguana-creators-localized-channels-ai-clone-voices/); [Tubefilter, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/06/24/linguana-content-localization-distribution-monetization-in-2026/)
- AIR's hybrid recommendation (2025-2026): use free auto-dub to test demand, then move proven languages to hybrid or pro dubbing; drop languages with low retention (one 80M-view channel dropped Hindi and Serbian); track "Casual + Regular" viewer share per language (under 20% after 4 months = problem; 40%+ = working) — [AIR](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/is-youtubes-ai-dubbing-safe-for-your-channel-auto-dub-vs-pro-with-real-retention-data); [AIR languages guide, Aug 2025](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/top-languages-to-localize-your-youtube-content-in-2025)
- YouTube creators can disable auto-dubbing channel-wide or set dubs to publish manually; guides on turning it off proliferated in 2026 — [gHacks, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/02/05/youtube-makes-auto-dubbing-available-to-all-creators-worldwide/); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15569972)
- Viewer complaints about no global opt-out (Apr 2025) and automatic title/description translation (Jul 2025) — [PPC Land, 18 May 2026](https://ppc.land/youtube-auto-dubbing-tops-6m-daily-viewers-expands-to-27-languages/)
- DittoDub founder: added-language views do not reduce the creator's English audience or brand-deal value — [Net Influencer, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.netinfluencer.com/inside-dittodub-the-ai-dubbing-startup-behind-erling-haaland-multilingual-youtube-push/)

### Inferences
- The partner's remaining moat is operational and commercial rather than technical: (1) the payee/operator position on a separate channel's AdSense (not shared with the main channel's financiers such as Spotter), (2) localized packaging and community management that YouTube's track feature does not provide, (3) a separate recommendation surface per language, and (4) aggregated multilingual inventory to sell to brands. Voice quality is a shrinking moat as ElevenLabs Dubbing v2 and YouTube Expressive Speech converge.
- Defensibility is weak at the technology layer (ElevenLabs, HeyGen, YouTube) and moderate at the relationship layer (agency referrals like Night→Linguana; "already established creators only"). Capital intensity is low (Linguana's $8.5M seed is the largest raise found in this niche), which also means low barriers to entry.

### Gaps
- No creator-interview or trade-press evidence found of a creator terminating a localization partner because of auto-dubbing, or of a creator leaving auto-dub for a partner after Feb 2026.
- No data on how auto-dubbed main-channel tracks affect views on a creator's pre-existing separate dubbed channels (cannibalization) — the single most important unanswered question for the model's durability.
